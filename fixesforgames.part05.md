### Speech
- **B-S1 Arrow-key lag with SAPI 5.** Symptom: speech lags when arrowing quickly, worst on some voices. Cause: SAPI's purge blocks while its own audio plays (FlexVoice2 Bill 350-710 ms). Fix: render each text into an SpMemoryStream (format 34) and play it through winmm waveOut; stop = waveOutReset (3 ms). Check: `key_latency_check.py`, `speech_latency_check.py`, `audible_latency_check.py`. Playbook 7.4.1. Found: Brain P8.
- **B-S2 Words skipped.** Cause: reading the memory stream while the engine still writes. Fix: read only after `WaitUntilDone` reports done; long texts sentence by sentence. Playbook 7.4.1.
- **B-S3 Real-time voices too slow to render.** Fix: per-voice speed probe; speak such voices directly through SAPI. Playbook 7.4.1.
- **B-S4 Silent voice names.** Cause: some voices make no audio for numbers or non-ASCII letters (BestSpeech Fred). Fix: retry the text written another way (comma between numbers, ASCII fold, digits spelled). Check: render every voice name once. Playbook 7.4.1.
- **B-S5 Voice list incomplete or huge.** Cause: registry-only enumeration misses voices (1,789 in SAPI, 249 in the registry). Fix: SAPI's own enumeration in the background; engine list, "all voices", type-ahead or first-letter jump. Playbook 7.4, 7.4.2.
- **B-S6 Two programs speaking through SAPI at once can deadlock.** Fix: 8 s no-progress watchdog, `WaitUntilDone(>0)` not 0 ms polls, never NVDA `speakSsml` blocking with no words. Playbook 7.4. Found: Brain S7.1.
- **B-S7 Labels spoken at every arrow key.** Fix: keep them short, hints in help. The owner's NVDA ran the voice at SAPI +8 (read `%APPDATA%\nvda\nvda.ini`); compare at the speakers. Playbook 7.4.1.

- **B-S10 NVDA: long silence after a prompt (end mark never arrives).** Symptom: 2 s of silence before the sounds. Cause: the SSML mark callback is an RPC callback delivered only while a BLOCKING `speakSsml` is open; asynchronous calls never get it, so the wait ran out its estimate. Fix: worker thread making blocking calls one after the other; the return of the call is the end of speech. Check: `prompt_gap_check.py nvda` (limit 150 ms; Brain 2,013 ms to 160 ms). Playbook 7.4.4. Owner-verified 2026-10-13 on the owner's PC (Brain 1.0.2, NVDA, SAPI 5 and JAWS): the pause after the prompt is gone.
- **B-S11 NVDA: a cancel while idle poisons the next call.** Symptom: every wait ended at once, sounds over the prompt. Cause: `cancelSpeech` with nothing speaking leaves a stale cancel; the next blocking call returns 1223 after milliseconds. Fix: cancel only when a call of ours is open, queued or held (also in close); retry once a call cancelled by nobody within 250 ms. Check: tests with a fake DLL that has the stale-cancel behavior. Playbook 7.4.4. Owner-verified 2026-10-13 on the owner's PC (Brain 1.0.2, NVDA, SAPI 5 and JAWS): the pause after the prompt is gone.
- **B-S12 NVDA hang guards.** A blocking call with no spoken words can hang 301 s: send text without a letter or digit as asynchronous SSML; cancel a call still open 2 s past the estimate. Playbook 7.4.4.

- **B-A15 The level of a playing sound changes in the mixer, not in the samples.** Symptom (design): the kit baked each sound's volume into its samples, right for Brain, whose sounds never change level while they play; nfl4's crowd 2 is ramped in 1 dB steps while it loops (and crowd 1 changes at a game's start and in F11), and a re-render would restart the loop. Cause, measured: SDL_mixer's chunk volume and channel volume each have 128 whole steps and multiply as whole numbers (about 1 dB error at -27 dB, a quiet sound at wavol -2000 becomes 0 or 1 step); its panning effect (`Channel.set_volume(left, right)`) is a float multiply with 255 steps. - **B-W4 TranslateMessage queues the character BEHIND keys that are already waiting, so a key and its character can come apart.** Symptom (nfl4 S7.1, a test): five WM_KEYDOWN posted to the window at once arrived as five keys and THEN five characters; a name prompt that takes "the character that follows this key" would have got another key or nothing. Cause: TranslateMessage posts the WM_CHAR to the end of the posted-message queue; with real typing the next key usually comes later, but a fast typist, held keys or typed-ahead keys during a synchronous line (B-S19) queue several keys before the loop runs. Check: nfl4 `game\tests\test_window.py::test_keys_that_type_are_followed_by_their_character_and_others_by_none` and `::test_typed_characters_reach_the_key_input_in_order_with_their_keys`.
