## B. Bugs and lessons, continued (part 15 is full; sections C and D are in part 16)

- **B-T150 Writing an e-mail in the owner's Gmail through the browser: a field reference goes stale and the typing becomes Gmail's keyboard shortcuts.** Symptom: nfl4 S-N (2026-11-07), the owner: "email the full link to the latest installer to Dennis". In one batch the agent clicked the To field, typed the address and Tab, then clicked the Subject and the body by the references found before: the recipient's chip had moved the compose window, so the subject text went into the To line and the body text went to the Gmail page, where its letters ran as shortcuts (a notification opened and marked read, two empty compose tabs from Shift+D, "/" put the rest into a search). Nothing was sent. Cause: references taken before a change of the page were used after it, and the keys were typed with no check of where the cursor was. Fix: one field at a time; find the field again after every change of the page; choose the recipient from Gmail's suggestion and read its address there; before typing, check that `document.activeElement` is the field (aria-label "Message Body", contentEditable); a body that starts empty must not start with "/" (Gmail's writing help); read the To, Subject and body back from the page before Send; after a slip, check Sent, Drafts and the inbox, close the stray tabs, discard the bad draft and put back what changed (mark unread). Check: Sent shows the one message with the right address, subject and link; the inbox's unread count is what it was. Found: nfl4, 2026-11-07.

- **B-S52 A radio call of a field goal names its length, as every real broadcast does.** Symptom: nfl4 0.16.20 (2026-11-07), a tester (GitHub issue 7): "have radio announcer tell you the distance of the field goal being attempted. It should be just like in a radio broadcast." The radio play-by-play turned the original's "<kicker> is in for the field goal attempt" into "Here comes <kicker> for the field goal attempt." with no length, although a real call always says it ("on for a 42-yard try") and the style guide had measured that the classic call talks about kicking range far more than the modern one. Cause: the calls were built from the shapes of the original's sentences, and that sentence holds no number, so nothing asked for one. Fix: the caller's rule for the set-up computes the length from the game state it already reads (the yards to the goal line + the hold's 7 + the end zone's 10, the same formula the game's own kick uses; the original's help says "add seventeen yards"), says it in every set-up variant, calls a try of 50 yards or more a long one at a higher excitement level, keeps it for the result (some result variants repeat it), forgets it at the next play's start, and leaves out the variants that need it when it is unknown; "an" before 8, 11, 18 and 80 to 89. The engine's sentence and the plain calls stay the original's words. In every sports game with radio calls: list the facts a real call gives at each moment (a kick's length, a punt's hang time and distance, the yard line, the down and distance, the clock near the end) and check that each call that can carry one does, from the state, never by changing the engine's sentence. Check: a whole game with a playbook compares every length said with the kick the game planned; a unit test of the yard lines (the defense's half, the own half, 18 and 80 yards); the same draws and scores with the calls on and off. Found: nfl4, 2026-11-09.

- **B-X165 A man brought back to the roster gets everything a man of the roster must have; a save that finds one value wrong mends it instead of refusing the whole file.** Symptom: nfl4 0.16.20 (2026-11-08), a tester (GitHub issue 8): "After activating someone off injured reserve it can't save the franchise." His log: after the activation every save failed the whole-file check ("the salary of player 1929 is 0"); the activation, a starter, a depth move and a lineup were each told as done while nothing was written, and "the franchise could not be saved" came only at the next game's start, so a whole session of changes was lost. Cause: the men the real rosters put on a reserve list start with no contract (it "ran with the list"); the roster cut gives them one when they come back, but the new activation (a second path back to the roster, added later) did not, and its tests only brought back men the test itself had placed, who kept theirs. Fix: the activation gives a man with no running contract the market contract and says it before Enter; the save mends any rostered man with no contract (the market price) and logs a warning naming the men, so one forgotten value never makes the whole save unwritable. In every game: (1) when a new path moves a thing into a state (a man to the roster, an item to the inventory, a team to the league), list what every thing in that state must have (the whole-file check is that list) and give it on the new path, the same way the older paths do; (2) test a new path with the real data's own records in every starting state (men who began on each list), not only with records the test made; (3) a save's check that would refuse the file first mends the values it can mend safely, with a warning in the log, and refuses only damage it cannot mend; (4) a step that says "done" after a save checks what the save returned. Check: a test activates a man of each real list, saves through the real store in strict mode and loads him back; a test saves a record with the value broken on purpose and finds it mended and logged; both fail with the fix switched off. Found: nfl4, 2026-11-09.

- **B-P223 A roster update that keeps a whole team for one count over a limit, on the day after a game.** Symptom: nfl4 0.16.20, GitHub issue 9: "It says 4 teams can't be updated", "consistently"; and in a franchise "My roster wasn't updated". Cause: the clubs' own roster pages are a day behind their moves after a game day (the players elevated from the practice squad still on the 53, a released man still on the squad), so 3 to 5 teams were over 53 active or 17 on the squad; the code that sent elevations back took EVERY man of the previous file's squad, not as many as the team was over, so a team whose two former squad men had been signed to the 53 got a squad of 18; one count over a limit then kept the whole team weeks old. And the franchise's update was designed never to touch the player's own team, which the player did not expect and the question did not let him change. Fix: read the team's transactions page only for a team over a limit and let each man's newest move decide (elevated, promoted, released, placed on a list); send back only min(over, 2, the squad's room) men, the named elevations first, never a man named as promoted; fit what is still over by the named releases and list moves, then hold back only the newest additions; and give the franchise's question a third answer that brings the player's own team up too, keeping his own chart choices. In every game that reads live rosters or any live list with limits: (1) a source a day behind is normal, so fit the excess with the source's own evidence and keep the rest, never throw away a whole team or record for one count; (2) a correction step moves only as many items as the limit is over, and never more than the other list has room for; (3) when a guess is needed, look for the source's own log of moves before guessing, and test the reader on the source's real sentences (names with Jr., initials, two men in one sentence, a plain "sign" with no list named); (4) when an update leaves the player's own things alone, the question says so and offers the other choice. Check: a whole read with three teams over a limit keeps none; eight mutations of the fix (work\i9_1109\mutate.py) each fail a test. Found: nfl4, 2026-11-09.

Check: a suite on a clean copy reports no FileNotFoundError and no failure in those four files. Found: nfl4, 2026-11-09.

- **B-W47 A menu item belongs beside the things it changes, not among the switches.** Symptom: nfl4 0.16.21 (GitHub issue 10, 2026-11-09): a tester wrote "Consider moving deapth Chart to either roster or Franchise. I think this would make more sense." The depth chart (who starts, the order of the backups, the key downs) was the 17th of 18 items of F9, Rules, after the broadcast and sound switches, while the roster it orders was in F9, Franchise; the roster's own items could make a man a starter but not move him in his order. Cause: each feature's item was added to the menu its session was building (the depth chart came with the rules' real players), never placed again when the menu it belonged to grew. Fix: the depth chart moved to the Franchise menu right after "your roster" (in a franchise: the player's own team) and to the end of that menu outside a franchise (any team, for single games), it left the Rules menu so that menu holds only rules, and a man's items in the roster got "his place on the depth chart", his position's order with the list started on him (`choose_flow(..., start_on=man)`) so the move keys work at once. In every game: (1) put an item where a player would look for the thing it changes: the people with the roster, the rules with the rules; when a menu gains a section that is the item's home, move it there and say so in the changelog with the new path; (2) a list of things (players, sounds, saves) gives a way from one thing to its own settings, the list started on that thing; (3) keep each move out of the saved data (the settings and the save keep their keys), so nothing set before is lost; (4) every help line, key row, README and generated guide that names the old path is changed in the same commit, and a test fails on the old path; (5) moving an item moves the tests that count Down to the items after it (B-T148): find them by the counting failures of one full run, change each count by one in a script that checks each replacement is found exactly as often as expected, and replace the longer count first where a changed line could match the next pattern (6 to 7 made a second 7). Check: tests\test_issue10_depth_chart_place.py (the Rules menu has 17 items and no depth chart; Franchise has it after the roster and last outside a franchise; the roster's item opens the order on the man and U moves him; no document names the old path). Found: nfl4, 2026-11-09.

- **B-W48 The word that snaps the ball is still the snap count: a timeout key pressed while it is said comes before the snap.** Symptom: nfl4 0.16.21 (2026-11-09, GitHub issue 11, JAWS): "I tried to call a timeout before the kicker kicked a Field goal. It wasn't granted." The problem report showed T 0.17 s after the holder's " hike! " was sent, while the word was still being said; the game logged it as a live play, kept it, the kick was good and the timeout was spent after it, before the kick-off. Cause: the kick's snap count made the kick live (the end of the dead-ball period, rule 0.22) before saying the word, so the live moment was the word's first sound; no one hears a word and presses a key within 0.17 s, so the player pressed before the snap. Fix: say the snap word, then make the play live; a key during the word cuts it, the timeout is called and the kick is withdrawn; a key after it is kept as before. Where the snap's own sound and draws start with the word (nfl4's scrimmage " Hike! ": the hike sound, the looping play noise, the carrier drawn at once) the play stays live from the word's start, and that choice is recorded. In every football game: (1) put the live moment after the snapper's or holder's call, not before it, where nothing of the play has started; (2) never add a pause for reaction time (silence before every kick, playbook 7.4.4); (3) the debug log writes the moment the ball became live (nfl4: ball_live with what and the down), so a report of a refused or late timeout shows which side of the snap the key was on; (4) test it by pressing the key inside the speech call of the word (wrap the speech manager's say in the test), not only at the pauses around it. Check: tests\test_issue11_kick_hike_timeout.py (field goal, the kicks of 2026, punt, kicked try, a refused key, the boundary after the word, the log line; 8 of 10 fail with the old order). Found: nfl4, 2026-11-09.

- **B-T153 exe_smoke's sound check fails when the PC plays other sound for its whole quiet wait.** Symptom: nfl4 0.16.23 (2026-11-09): "FAIL a sound plays (loopback): baseline peak 0.99330, peak during playback 0.99504" in four of seven runs, both bits, while every other check passed; the same exe passed minutes later with a baseline of 0.0002. Cause: the check compares the speakers' loopback during the game's start with a baseline taken after waiting up to 15 s for one quiet second; the owner's PC was playing other sound (a browser, a screen reader reading) for longer than that, so the baseline was already near full scale and no game sound could be three times louder. Not the game. Fix: read the baseline in the line: above 0.005 the measure is void, not a failure of the build; check that no earlier smoke run's nfl4.exe is still alive, then run that bit and mode again until the baseline is quiet, and publish only when every bit and mode has an ALL PASS run (rule 0.37); the commit of the build says how many runs were void. Check: each published build's work folder has one ALL PASS file per bit and mode. Found: nfl4, 2026-11-09.

- **B-T154 Reading a debug log for a sentence said twice: look at the key just before the run of speech first; an F2 replay repeats the last play's sentences on purpose.** Symptom: nfl4, the second review of GitHub issue 11 (2026-11-10): the log showed " time out called by the Panthers. " and " they have 1 time out left. " twice within two seconds, which looked like a fault of rule 0.28 (3). Cause: the tester pressed F2 between them; the replay sends the whole last play again, every sentence queued within a few milliseconds of the key's pump line, and that is the replay the player asked for. Fix: none in the game. When a report or a log seems to say a sentence twice, find the key lines (`key ... down` and `pump`) just before the second copy: F2 (or the game's replay key) means a replay; a second copy with no key before it, or spaced like live speech, is the bug rule 0.28 (3) forbids, and its test is the whole-game speech test. Also: a crash log's single hang in a voice's first call (a SAPI probe that waited 10 s once in 408 launches) is recorded in the review, not fixed, unless it comes back; the start-up wait is already capped (nfl4: 15 s in sapi5.start). Check: the review of a report names, for each sentence said twice, the key before it. Found: nfl4, 2026-11-10.

- **B-P224 A choice made before a play is asked again whenever the play is withdrawn or played again; a "play again" path goes by the side with the ball.** Symptom: nfl4 0.16.23 (2026-11-10, GitHub issue 12, JAWS): "when going for 2 points if a penalty or timeout is called you don't get the choice to go for 2 or kick the extra point." The log: the two-point try chosen, a flag on the try accepted, "the try will be played again from the 7 yard line", then the play menu at once; a timeout before the try's snap did the same. Cause: the choice of the try (kick or two) was asked once, after the touchdown; the timeout's way back (where a withdrawn play returns) and the penalty's "try again" step both went to the play menu, and only the kick had a way back to the choice. The "try again" step also went to the player's offense menu when the computer had the ball. NFL 11-3-1 (2026): after the ball is ready, the offense may change its choice of try after a penalty or a charged timeout by either team. Fix: (1) every way back after a withdrawn or replayed play (the timeout's back in the menu, in the snap count and in a kick's set-up; the penalty's continuation) goes to the last CHOICE the rules let the player change, not to the play menu below it; (2) the choice asked again starts on what he chose, so Enter keeps it, and keeps what the penalty set (the spot); (3) a continuation step that replays a down asks which side has the ball and gives the player his own side's menu; (4) a flag the timeout rules read (nfl4: the try's down still to come at 0:00) is set again when the choice is asked again; (5) log the replay and the choice made again. Check: tests\test_issue12_try_choice_again.py (8; 7 fail with the old code): T in the try's menu and in its snap count, the penalty's continuation for each side, the kick chosen instead, a timeout at 0:00 after that, the first choice unchanged, the log lines. Found: nfl4, 2026-11-10.
