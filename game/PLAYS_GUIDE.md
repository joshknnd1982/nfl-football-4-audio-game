# Plays guide

What every play, menu item, list and setting of the game does, in plain words for a person who has never watched football. In a play menu of the N. F. L. rules, press D to hear the description of the current item; in Classic, F7 reads the descriptions of the original's plays. These sentences are the game's own text, and the plays are football ideas named in plain football words. The README has the full notice about the plays and about trademarks.

## The original's plays

Offense menu:

- **Line Plunge**: The running back runs straight ahead into the line for a short gain. Best when you need only a yard or so.
- **End Run**: The running back runs toward the side line and tries to turn up the field. It can gain a lot, or lose yards if defenders are waiting.
- **Screen Pass**: A short pass to a running back or receiver behind the line of scrimmage, with blockers in front. Works if the defense rushes; risky if it is ready.
- **Short Pass**: A pass thrown a short distance. Easier to complete than a long pass, but it gains fewer yards.
- **Long Pass**: A pass thrown far down the field. It can gain many yards, but it is harder to complete and may be intercepted.
- **Field Goal**: A kick through the goal post for three points. Choose it on fourth down when you are close enough.
- **Punt**: A kick that sends the ball away to the other team, usually on fourth down when you cannot gain the yards.

Defense menu:

- **Goal Line**: Extra players crowd the line to stop a run close to the goal line. Weak against passes.
- **Short Run**: The defense is set to stop a run for a short gain, such as a yard or two. Weak against long passes.
- **Long Run**: The defense is set to stop a run that goes for many yards, such as a run toward the side line. Weak against short runs.
- **Blitz**: Extra players rush the quarterback. If it works, the offense loses yards; if not, the pass can go a long way.
- **Short Pass**: Defenders are set to stop short passes. Weak against long passes and runs.
- **Long Pass**: Defenders drop back to stop long passes. Weak against runs and short passes.

## Menu words

- **Back**: Goes back to the list of play groups.
- **More Plays**: Opens the other lists of plays that are not in the first menu, such as play action and trick plays.
- **Favourites**: Opens the plays you have chosen as favourites, so you can call them quickly.
- **Again**: Calls the same play you called last time.
- **Find a Play by Name**: Lets you type the first letters of a play's name to find it.
- **Formation and Tempo**: Opens the settings for how the offense or defense lines up and how fast the offense gets ready.
- **Time Out**: Stops the clock for a time out; you have three in each half. You can also press T at any time before the play starts.
- **Challenge the Ruling**: Asks the referee to look at the video of the last play. A challenge that loses costs a time out.
- **Save Game**: Saves the game so you can finish it later.
- **More Plays (on defense)**: Opens every list of defensive calls, such as blitzes and zone coverages, and the situation defenses.

## The groups of a playbook menu

With a playbook of the N. F. L. rules, the offense menu starts with these groups; Enter opens one and Backspace or Left goes back.

- **Inside Runs**: Runs straight ahead through the middle of the line for short, steady gains.
- **Outside Runs**: Runs toward the side line that try to get around the end of the line.
- **Screens**: Short passes to a runner or receiver with blockers in front, to beat a pass rush.
- **Short Passes**: Short passes that are easier to complete but gain fewer yards.
- **Deep Passes**: Long passes down the field, with big gains and bigger risks.
- **Play Action**: Pass plays that begin with a fake handoff.
- **Quarterback Runs**: Plays where the quarterback runs with the ball.

## Offense: when your team has the ball

### Inside Runs

Runs straight ahead through the middle of the line for short, steady gains.

- **Inside Zone**: A handoff to the running back, who runs between the guards and picks the hole that opens. Steady gains against two deep defenders; weak when many defenders crowd the line.
- **Dive**: A fast handoff straight ahead through the middle. It gains a short, sure yard or two against a blitz, but loses ground when defenders crowd the line.
- **Draw Play**: The quarterback fakes a pass, then hands the ball off. Works against a blitz; weak when many defenders wait near the line or a defender stays back.
- **Split Zone**: A handoff up the middle while a blocker cuts across to stop the defender coming from behind. Good against a thin defense; extra rushers can stop it.
- **Mid Zone**: A run just outside the guard, with blockers moving sideways together and the runner choosing a hole. Good against two deep defenders; defenders slanting across the line spoil it.
- **Delayed Draw**: The quarterback fakes a pass, waits a moment, then hands off. Works against a blitz; weak when a defender stays back to watch the runner.
- **Lead Draw**: A fake pass, then a handoff to a runner with a big back leading the way. Strong against a blitz; stopped when many defenders crowd the line.
- **Wham**: A surprise blocker hits the middle defender while the runner goes up the middle. Beats extra rushers; weak against big defenders who hold their ground.
- **Belly**: A handoff with the runner following a lead blocker just off the middle. Good against a thin defense; weak when the defense squeezes the line tight.
- **Fullback Dive**: A short handoff straight ahead to a big running back, mostly for one yard near the goal line. Reliable against a blitz; stops when defenders crowd the line.

### Power and Gap Runs

Runs where blockers lead the way or hit a defender from the side.

- **Power Left**: Linemen run to the side to lead the way and the runner follows them. Strong against a thin defense; defenders slanting into the hole can stop it.
- **Counter**: The play starts one way, then the blockers and the runner cut back the other way. Beats rushers who chase the first move; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Iso**: One blocker clears a defender and the runner follows straight into the hole. Good against two deep defenders; extra rushers stop it.
- **Trap**: A defender is let through on purpose and hit from the side while the runner goes up the middle. Beats rushers; weak against big defenders who hold their ground.
- **Lead Run**: A running back leads the way and the runner follows him through the hole. Good against a thin defense; weak when defenders crowd the line.
- **Duo**: Two blockers push the same defender apart while the runner picks a hole. Good against two deep defenders; extra rushers can break it up.
- **Counter Trey**: Like the counter, with two linemen leading the runner back against the flow. Beats extra rushers; weak when defenders stay wide.
- **Fullback Trap**: A big back runs through the middle behind a surprise block. Beats extra rushers; weak when defenders crowd the line.
- **Off Tackle**: A run toward the outer side of the line. Good against a thin defense; weak when the defense squeezes the line in.
- **Cutback Run**: The runner heads outside, then cuts back against the flow. Good against defenders who slant to one side; weak against big defenders who hold the middle.

### Outside Runs

Runs toward the side line that try to get around the end of the line.

- **Stretch Outside**: The line moves sideways, the runner heads toward the sideline, then turns up the field. Good against two deep defenders; defenders who stay wide stop it.
- **Outside Zone**: Blockers all move toward the sideline and the runner picks a lane. Good against a thin defense; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Toss Sweep**: The quarterback tosses the ball to a running back who runs wide behind blockers. Works when the middle is crowded; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Pitch**: A quick toss to a running back running wide. Beats a blitz; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Quick Toss**: A very fast toss to the running back moving toward the sideline. Beats extra rushers; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Crack Toss**: A toss wide while a receiver blocks inside on the first defender. Works when the middle is crowded; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Pin and Pull Sweep**: Some linemen block the nearest defenders while others run ahead to lead the runner around the end. Good against a thin defense; slanting defenders can stop it.
- **Double Pull Sweep**: Two linemen run ahead of the runner around the end. Good against two deep defenders; extra rushers break it up.

### Misdirection Runs

Runs that start one way and then go another to fool the defense.

- **Jet Sweep**: A receiver runs across before the snap and takes a quick handoff, racing around the end. Works against man for man defense; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **End Around**: A receiver takes a handoff going the other way and runs around the end. Surprises extra rushers; loses yards if defenders stay wide.
- **Reverse**: The runner goes one way, then hands the ball to a receiver going the other way. Surprises extra rushers; slow to develop, so defenders who stay wide stop it.
- **Fake Reverse**: The runner pretends to hand the ball to a receiver going the other way, then keeps it. Good against defenders who stay wide; slanting defenders can get through.
- **Orbit Sweep**: A receiver circles behind the quarterback before the snap and takes a quick handoff. Works against man for man defense; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Jet Fake Inside Zone**: A receiver runs across as if to take a quick handoff, but the runner takes the ball up the middle. Works against man for man defense; weak when the line is crowded.

### Screens

Short passes to a runner or receiver with blockers in front, to beat a pass rush.

- **Running Back Screen**: The quarterback drops back, lets the rushers come, then throws short to a running back with blockers in front. Strong against a blitz; weak when defenders drop back.
- **Receiver Screen**: A short throw to a receiver near the sideline with blockers ahead of him. Good against two deep defenders; defenders who bump the receivers can stop it.
- **Tight End Screen**: A short throw to a tight end with blockers ahead of him. Works against a blitz; weak when defenders drop back and are ready.
- **Tunnel Screen**: A short throw to a receiver who runs behind a wall of blockers. Works against man for man defense; weak when defenders drop back.
- **Bubble Screen**: A very quick throw to a receiver near the sideline with blockers in front. Works when the middle is crowded; defenders who bump the receivers can stop it.
- **Flat Screen**: A short throw to a back running toward the sideline. Works against a blitz; weak against defenders guarding areas of the field.
- **Middle Screen**: A short throw over the middle to a running back with blockers waiting. Works against a blitz; weak when defenders drop back.
- **Jailbreak Screen**: A throw to a receiver near the sideline while blockers race out ahead of him. Works against a heavy blitz; defenders who bump the receivers can stop it.
- **Fullback Screen**: A short throw to a big running back with blockers ahead of him. Works against a blitz; weak when defenders drop back.
- **Shovel Pass**: The quarterback flips the ball forward to a runner close by. Works against a blitz; a defender who watches the runner can stop it.

### Quick Passes

Short, fast passes thrown before the defense can rush.

- **Hitch Routes**: Receivers run a few steps, stop and turn back to the quarterback. A short, safe catch against two deep defenders; defenders who bump the receivers break it up.
- **Quick Out**: A receiver runs a few steps, then cuts toward the sideline. Good against defenders guarding areas; defenders who bump the receivers break it up.
- **Stick**: A short throw to a receiver who stops quickly near the line, with other short choices. Good against a blitz and defenders guarding areas; man for man defense can cover it.
- **Slants**: Receivers cut sharply across the field toward the middle for a fast throw. Beats a blitz because the ball leaves quickly; defenders who drop back can cover it.
- **Spacing**: Receivers spread across the field with short patterns so one is open. Good against defenders guarding areas; man for man defense can cover it.
- **Slant Flat**: One receiver slants across the middle while another runs to the sideline, and the quarterback throws to the open one. Good against one deep defender; weak against two.
- **Snag**: Three receivers run a short stop, a corner pattern and a sideline pattern, giving the quarterback several choices. Good against defenders guarding areas; man for man defense covers it.
- **Speed Out**: A receiver runs hard, then breaks quickly toward the sideline. Good against defenders guarding areas; defenders who bump the receivers can cover it.
- **Fade**: A receiver runs down the sideline and the quarterback throws a high ball. Good against one deep defender; two deep defenders can cover it.
- **Back Shoulder Fade**: The quarterback throws the ball behind the receiver so only he can catch it. Good against defenders who bump the receivers; two deep defenders can cover it.

### Crossers and Backs

Passes to receivers running across the field and to running backs.

- **Curl and Flat**: One receiver runs and turns back while another runs to the sideline, so the quarterback has two choices. Good against one deep defender; weak against two.
- **Drag**: A receiver runs shallow across the field. Beats a blitz because the ball comes out fast; defenders who drop back can cover it.
- **Mesh Crossers**: Two receivers cross close to each other so the defenders get in each other's way. Good against man for man defense; weak when defenders drop back.
- **Shallow Cross**: Receivers cross the field close to the line. Beats a blitz; weak when defenders drop back.
- **Drive**: One receiver crosses the field close to the line and another crosses deeper. Good against one deep defender; weak against two.
- **Angle Route**: A running back runs forward, then cuts back toward the middle. Good against man for man defense; defenders guarding areas can cover it.
- **Swing Pass**: A running back runs toward the sideline and catches a short throw. Beats a blitz; defenders guarding areas can stop it.
- **Check Down**: When nobody is open, the quarterback throws short to a running back. Safe against two deep defenders; man for man defense can cover it.
- **Flat Route**: A receiver runs a short path to the sideline. Good against one deep defender; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Whip Route**: A receiver fakes toward the sideline and cuts back inside. Good against man for man defense; defenders guarding areas can cover it.

### Intermediate Passes

Passes of medium length, usually over the middle or toward the sideline.

- **Dig**: A receiver runs deep, then cuts across the middle. Good against one deep defender, but the quarterback needs time and two deep defenders can cover it.
- **Levels**: Receivers run across the field at different depths, so one defender cannot cover them all. Good against defenders guarding areas; weak against man for man defense.
- **Y Cross**: The tight end crosses the middle while other receivers go deep or wide. Good against one deep defender; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Smash**: One receiver runs a short stop and another runs deep toward the corner. Good against two deep defenders; weak against one deep defender.
- **Curl**: A receiver runs deeper than on a short stop, then turns back to the quarterback. Good against defenders guarding areas; defenders who bump the receivers can cover it.
- **Comeback**: A receiver runs deep, then comes back toward the sideline. Good against man for man defense; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Out Route**: A receiver runs a medium distance, then turns toward the sideline. Good against defenders guarding areas; defenders who bump the receivers can cover it.
- **Deep Out**: A receiver runs deep, then cuts to the sideline. Good against one deep defender; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Choice Route**: The receiver decides while running whether to stop, cut out or slant, depending on the defender. Good against man for man defense; weak when defenders drop back.
- **Sail**: Three receivers run to different depths on one side, giving the quarterback several choices. Good against one deep defender; two deep defenders can cover it.

### Deep Passes

Long passes down the field, with big gains and bigger risks.

- **Four Verticals**: Four receivers run straight down the field and the quarterback picks the open one. Strong against one deep defender; weak against defenders staying very deep, and the quarterback needs time.
- **Deep Post**: A receiver runs deep, then cuts toward the middle of the field. Good against two deep defenders; weak against one deep defender.
- **Corner Route**: A receiver runs deep, then cuts toward the far corner of the field. Good against two deep defenders; weak against man for man defense.
- **Go Route**: A receiver simply runs straight down the field for a long throw. Good against man for man defense; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Deep Cross**: A receiver crosses the field far from the line. Good against man for man defense; weak when defenders drop back.
- **Flood**: Three receivers run to different depths on the same side of the field. Good against one deep defender; weak against man for man defense.
- **Wheel Route**: A running back runs toward the sideline, then turns up the field for a long throw. Good against man for man defense; weak against defenders guarding areas.
- **Seam**: Receivers run straight up the middle between the deep defenders. Good against one deep defender; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Post Corner**: A receiver fakes toward the middle, then cuts to the corner. Good against one deep defender; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Double Posts**: Two receivers run deep toward the middle from both sides. Good against two deep defenders; weak against one deep defender.

### Deep Combinations

Plays where two or three receivers run different long patterns.

- **Dagger**: One receiver clears defenders by going deep while another cuts across behind them. Good against one deep defender; two deep defenders can cover it.
- **Mills**: Two receivers cut toward the middle at different depths. Good against two deep defenders; weak against one deep defender.
- **Yankee**: A deep cut toward the middle and a deep crossing pattern together, a long play that needs time. Good against one deep defender; two deep defenders can cover it.
- **Divide**: Two receivers run deep and split apart, one up the middle and one toward the sideline. Good against one deep defender; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Scissors**: Two receivers cross on deep patterns, one to the corner and one to the middle. Good against two deep defenders; weak against man for man defense.
- **Post Wheel**: One receiver runs toward the middle while a running back circles toward the sideline, then up the field. Good against man for man defense; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Sluggo**: A receiver fakes a short stop, then sprints deep. Beats defenders who bump the receivers; weak against two deep defenders, and hard to throw accurately.
- **Stutter Go**: A receiver stutters his steps to fool the defender, then runs deep. Good against man for man defense; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Out and Up**: A receiver fakes toward the sideline, then turns deep. Good against man for man defense; weak against two deep defenders.

### Play Action

Pass plays that begin with a fake handoff.

- **Play Action Short**: The quarterback fakes a handoff, then throws short. Works when defenders crowd the line to stop the run; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Play Action Deep**: A fake handoff, then a long throw. Good when defenders crowd the line or only one stays deep; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Play Action Shot**: A fake handoff draws defenders forward, then comes a long throw. A big gain if it works; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Play Action Cross**: A fake handoff, then a throw to a receiver crossing the field. Good against one deep defender; weak when defenders drop back.
- **Play Action Flood**: A fake handoff, then receivers run to three depths on one side. Good against one deep defender; weak against man for man defense.
- **Play Action Wheel**: A fake handoff, then a running back runs toward the sideline and up the field. Good against man for man defense; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Play Action Screen**: A fake handoff, then a short throw behind blockers. Beats a blitz; weak when defenders drop back.
- **Play Action Leak**: A fake handoff, then a tight end slips out to the side unnoticed. Good when defenders crowd the line; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Play Action Dig**: A fake handoff, then a receiver crosses the field. Good against one deep defender; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Play Action Corner**: A fake handoff, then a receiver cuts to the corner. Good against two deep defenders; weak against man for man defense.

### Moving Pocket

Pass plays where the quarterback runs to the side before he throws.

- **Bootleg Short**: The quarterback fakes a handoff, runs the other way and throws short on the move. Beats rushers who chase the run; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Rollout**: The quarterback runs to the side before he throws. Beats a blitz; defenders who stay wide can stop it.
- **Naked Bootleg**: The quarterback fakes a handoff, then runs the other way alone with no blockers ahead. Beats rushers who chase the run; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Waggle**: The quarterback fakes a handoff and moves to one side while receivers cross in front. Works against slanting defenders; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Sprint Out**: The quarterback runs fast toward the sideline and throws. Works against man for man defense; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Max Protect Shot**: Most of the offense stays in to block while only two receivers run deep. Good against a blitz; weak against two deep defenders.

### Quarterback Runs

Plays where the quarterback runs with the ball.

- **Quarterback Sneak**: The quarterback pushes forward behind the center for a yard or so. Reliable for a very short distance; fails against a crowded line.
- **Quarterback Keeper**: The quarterback keeps the ball and runs outside. Works against man for man defense; a defender who watches the quarterback can stop it.
- **Quarterback Draw**: The quarterback fakes a pass, then runs up the middle. Works against a blitz and man for man defense; a defender who watches the quarterback can stop it.
- **Quarterback Power**: The quarterback runs behind lead blockers up the middle. Good against a thin defense; a defender who watches the quarterback can stop it.
- **Sneak Push**: The quarterback sneaks forward while teammates push him from behind. Good against slanting defenders; weak against big defenders who hold their ground.
- **Bootleg Run**: The quarterback fakes a handoff, then runs around the end himself. Beats rushers who chase the run; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Quarterback Counter**: The quarterback fakes one way and runs the other with linemen leading. Beats the run blitz; a defender who watches the quarterback can stop it.
- **Quarterback Zone**: The quarterback runs wide while the blockers move sideways together. Good against two deep defenders; a defender who watches the quarterback can stop it.
- **Quarterback Crack Sweep**: The quarterback runs wide while a receiver blocks inside. Good when the middle is crowded; weak against defenders who stay wide.

### Option Runs

Runs where the quarterback chooses at the last moment to keep the ball or give it away.

- **Read Option Keep**: The quarterback watches one defender and either keeps the ball or hands it off, depending on that defender's move. Beats the run blitz; weak against a defender who watches the quarterback.
- **Speed Option**: The quarterback runs wide with a running back beside him and keeps the ball or pitches it, depending on the nearest defender. Beats a blitz; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Zone Read**: The quarterback sees what one defender does, then keeps the ball or hands it to the running back. Good against slanting defenders; defenders who chase along the line spoil it.
- **Triple Option**: The quarterback can hand off, keep the ball, or pitch it to a running back, choosing by watching defenders. Beats the run blitz; weak against a defender who covers the pitch.
- **Midline Option**: The quarterback hands off up the middle or keeps the ball, depending on one defender. Good against slanting defenders; defenders who chase along the line spoil it.
- **Inside Veer**: The quarterback hands the ball inside or keeps it going outside, depending on one defender. Beats the run blitz; defenders who chase along the line spoil it.
- **Inverted Veer**: The running back goes outside and the quarterback goes inside, and one defender decides who keeps the ball. Good against slanting defenders; weak against a defender who watches the quarterback.
- **Power Read**: A run with lead blockers, where the quarterback may keep the ball. Good against defenders who stay wide; a defender who watches the quarterback can stop it.
- **Receiver Pitch Option**: The quarterback runs wide and either keeps the ball or pitches it to a receiver. Beats a blitz; weak against a defender who covers the pitch.

### Run Pass Options

Plays that are a run and a quick pass at once. The quarterback chooses after the snap.

- **Run Pass Option**: A run and a quick pass at once: after the snap the quarterback watches one defender and hands off or throws. Strong against a crowded line; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Bubble Option**: A run play with a quick screen as the other choice: the quarterback hands off or throws behind the line. Good against a crowded line; defenders who bump the receivers can stop it.
- **Glance Option**: A run play with a quick slanting pass as the other choice. Good against the run blitz; weak against two deep defenders.
- **Quick Flat Option**: A run play with a short pass to the sideline as the other choice. Good against a crowded line; defenders who bump the receivers can stop it.
- **Stick Option**: A run play with a quick short pass as the other choice. Good against defenders guarding areas; weak against man for man defense.
- **Tight End Option**: A run play with a pass to a tight end as the other choice. Good against a crowded line; weak against defenders guarding areas.
- **Jet Option**: A receiver runs across before the snap, then the quarterback hands off or throws a quick screen. Good against man for man defense; weak against defenders who stay wide.
- **Alert Throw**: A run play where the quarterback may throw deep to a receiver left alone. Strong against a crowded line; weak against two deep defenders.

### Trick Plays

Unusual plays meant to fool the defense. They are rare and risky.

- **Flea Flicker**: A handoff, then the runner tosses the ball back to the quarterback for a deep throw. Strong against a crowded line, but risky and rarely used.
- **Halfback Pass**: A running back takes a handoff and throws downfield. Works against the run blitz; rarely used and risky because a runner is not a passer.
- **Double Pass**: The quarterback laterals to a receiver, who throws deep. Works when the line is crowded; rarely used and risky.
- **Throwback to the Quarterback**: A receiver catches a pass, then throws back across the field to the quarterback. Works against man for man defense; rarely used and risky.
- **Hook and Lateral**: A short catch followed by a lateral to a teammate who runs on. A desperate play to gain extra yards; very risky because of fumbles.
- **Statue of Liberty**: The quarterback pretends to pass, and a runner takes the ball from his raised hand. Works against a blitz; rarely used.
- **Jet Touch Pass**: A receiver in motion takes a quick short pass like a handoff. Works against man for man defense; rarely used.
- **Fake Sneak Swing**: The quarterback fakes a sneak, then throws to a back at the sideline. Works against a crowded line; rarely used.
- **Double Screen**: Two screens at once: blockers set up on both sides and the quarterback picks one side to throw to. Works against a blitz; rarely used.
- **Reverse Pass**: A receiver takes a handoff going the other way, then throws deep. Works against the run blitz; rarely used and risky.

### Goal Line and Short Yardage

Plays for when only a few yards are needed or the goal line is close.

- **Goal Line Fade**: A high throw to a receiver in the corner of the end zone. Works against man for man defense; weak when two defenders stay deep.
- **Rub Routes**: Receivers cross very close to each other so defenders collide. Works against man for man defense near the goal line; weak against defenders guarding areas.
- **Jumbo Power**: Extra blockers line up and push straight ahead. A strong run for a very short distance near the goal line; the run blitz can stop it.
- **Tight End Flat**: A fake handoff, then a short throw to a tight end moving toward the sideline. Works when the line is crowded; weak against defenders who stay wide.

### Clock Plays

Plays that stop or run the clock, and last chance throws.

- **Kneel Down**: The quarterback takes the snap and kneels to end the play safely. Used to run out the clock when winning; loses about a yard.
- **Spike the Ball**: The quarterback throws the ball into the ground to stop the clock. It uses a down, but saves time when you are behind.
- **Fake Spike**: The quarterback pretends to spike the ball, then throws a real pass. Works if the defense relaxes; rarely used.
- **Victory Formation Pass**: The team lines up as if to run out the clock, then the quarterback throws deep at once, without faking a kneel, which would end the play. A rare surprise, and risky.
- **Hail Mary**: A very long, high throw into a crowd of players in the end zone, tried when time is almost out. It rarely works, but it is the last chance.
- **Laterals at the End**: Players keep tossing the ball backward to gain yards when time is almost out. Very risky, since one fumble loses the ball, so a last chance only.

### Old Formations

Plays from old fashioned lineups, which are now rare.

- **Wishbone Dive**: An old formation with three running backs; one runs straight ahead through the middle. Strong against two deep defenders; weak when the line is crowded; rarely used.
- **Flexbone Option**: An old formation where the quarterback hands off, keeps the ball or pitches it. Works against the run blitz; rarely used, and weak against a defender who covers the pitch.
- **Single Wing Power**: A very old formation where a running back takes the snap and runs behind blockers. Works against a thin defense; rarely used.
- **Wing T Buck Sweep**: An old formation where a back runs wide behind blockers. Works against a thin defense; rarely used.
- **Full House Dive**: Three running backs line up behind the quarterback and one runs straight ahead. Works against two deep defenders; rarely used.
- **Wildcat Run**: A running back takes the snap and runs, with the quarterback lined up wide. Works against man for man defense; weak when the line is crowded; rarely used.
- **Wildcat Pass**: A running back takes the snap and throws a pass. Works when defenders crowd the line; rarely used and risky.
- **Split T Option**: An old play where the quarterback hands off, keeps the ball or pitches it. Beats a blitz; weak against a defender who covers the pitch.

### More Routes

More short and medium passes.

- **Return Route**: A receiver runs out, then comes back to the quarterback. A short, safe catch against man for man defense; defenders guarding areas can cover it.
- **Bench Route**: A receiver runs a medium distance toward the sideline. Good against defenders guarding areas; weak against defenders who bump the receivers.
- **Zig Route**: A receiver fakes toward the sideline, then cuts the other way. Good against man for man defense; defenders guarding areas can cover it.
- **Double Slants**: Two receivers cut sharply across the field for a fast throw. Beats a blitz; defenders who drop back can cover it.

## Defense: when the other team has the ball

### Goal Line Defenses

Defenses that crowd the line to stop runs close to the goal line.

- **Goal Line Eight Up**: Eight defenders crowd the line and the rest cover man for man with no deep help. Strong against runs near the goal line; a pass over their heads can score.
- **Goal Line Stack**: A packed line with five rushers. Strong against runs near the goal line; weak against play action passes.
- **Bear Front**: Eight defenders close to the line, with extra rushers coming. Strong against runs; weak against screens.
- **Goal Line Six Two**: Six linemen and two linebackers fill the line. Strong against runs near the goal line; weak against passes.
- **Goal Line Five Three**: Five linemen and three linebackers fill the line. Strong against runs near the goal line; weak against passes.
- **Short Yardage Four Four**: Four linemen and four linebackers crowd the line. Strong against runs for a yard or two; weak against passes.
- **Goal Line Man**: Defenders cover each receiver man for man while others rush. Strong against passes near the goal line; weak against runs.

### Run Fronts

Defenses set to stop runs, with different numbers of players near the line.

- **Base Run Fit**: The normal defense with four linemen, three linebackers and four defensive backs, each stopping part of the line. Strong against runs; weak against play action passes.
- **Inside Run Force**: Defenders squeeze inward to stop runs up the middle and force the runner toward the sideline. Strong against runs; weak against bootlegs.
- **Four Three Over**: Four linemen and three linebackers, with the line shifted toward the strong side of the offense. Strong against runs; weak against passes.
- **Four Three Under**: Four linemen and three linebackers, with the line shifted away from the strong side. Strong against runs; weak against passes.
- **Three Four Odd**: Three linemen and four linebackers, with a big lineman opposite the center. Strong against runs; weak against screens.
- **Tite Front**: Five defensive backs, with the linemen squeezed close together. Strong against runs; weak against draw plays.
- **Forty Six Defense**: Eight defenders crowd the line and extra rushers come. Strong against runs; weak against passes.
- **Five Two**: Five linemen and two linebackers. Strong against runs; weak against passes.
- **Six One**: Six linemen and one linebacker. Strong against runs; weak against passes.
- **Big Nickel**: Five defensive backs, with the extra one a big tackler in place of a linebacker. Strong against runs; weak against passes.

### Run Stunts

Defenses where the linemen move at the snap to stop a run.

- **Run Blitz**: Extra defenders charge at the runner. Strong against runs; weak against screens.
- **Slant Line**: The linemen slant to one side at the snap. Strong against runs; weak against counter plays.
- **Angle Line**: Linemen cut across at angles to confuse the blockers. Strong against runs; weak against counter plays.
- **Pinch Line**: Linemen squeeze toward the middle. Strong against runs up the middle; weak against runs outside.
- **Spill the Run**: Defenders push the run toward the sideline, where others wait. Strong against runs inside; weak against runs outside.
- **Box the Run**: Defenders hold wide to keep the runner inside. Strong against runs outside; weak against runs inside.
- **Two Gap Front**: Each lineman holds his ground and controls two spots on the line. Strong against runs; weak against draw plays.
- **One Gap Charge**: Each lineman charges at one spot on the line. Strong against runs; weak against draw plays.

### Edge and Contain

Defenses that keep the ball carrier or quarterback from getting around the end.

- **Contain Outside Force**: Outside defenders keep the ball carrier from getting around the end. Strong against runs outside; weak against runs up the middle.
- **Edge Setting**: The outside defenders hold their ground and make the runner turn back inside. Strong against runs outside; weak against runs up the middle.
- **Spy**: One defender watches the quarterback and follows him if he runs. Strong against quarterback runs; weak against passes.
- **Wide Nine**: The end defenders line up wide for a fast rush. Strong against passes; weak against runs.
- **Quarterback Contain**: Defenders keep the quarterback from running outside. Strong against plays where the quarterback chooses to keep the ball or give it away; weak against runs.
- **Pitch Assignment**: One defender is assigned to cover the pitch. Strong against plays where the quarterback may pitch the ball; weak against passes.
- **Scrape Exchange**: Linebackers slide along the line to follow the ball. Strong against plays where the quarterback may keep the ball; weak against runs.

### Blitzes

Defenses where extra players rush the quarterback.

- **Zone Blitz**: Some defenders rush while linemen drop back to guard areas. Strong against passes; weak against screens.
- **Cover Zero Blitz**: Six rushers, with every receiver covered man for man and no deep help. Strong against passes if it gets to the quarterback, but a long gain if it fails; weak against screens.
- **Safety Blitz**: A deep defender charges at the quarterback. Strong against passes; weak against screens.
- **Corner Blitz**: A cornerback charges at the quarterback from the outside. Strong against passes; weak against screens.
- **Linebacker Blitz**: Linebackers charge at the quarterback or runner. Strong against runs; weak against screens.
- **A Gap Blitz**: Linebackers charge through the holes next to the center. Strong against a running quarterback; weak against screens.
- **Double A Gap**: Two linebackers rush through the holes on each side of the center. Strong against passes; weak against screens.
- **Nickel Blitz**: The extra defensive back rushes the quarterback. Strong against passes; weak against screens.
- **Dime Blitz**: A defensive back rushes from a defense with six defensive backs. Strong against passes; weak against runs.
- **Defensive Back Fire**: A defensive back and others rush, with all receivers covered man for man. Strong against passes; weak against screens.

### Pressure Looks

Defenses that look like a blitz or confuse the blockers.

- **Fire Zone**: Five rushers while others guard areas. Strong against passes; weak against screens.
- **Overload Blitz**: Rushers come from one side to overpower the blockers there. Strong against passes; weak against screens.
- **Simulated Pressure**: It looks like a blitz but only four rush, while the others drop back. Strong against passes; weak against runs.
- **Creeper**: A linebacker looks like a rusher but drops back, and a lineman covers instead. Strong against passes; weak against runs.
- **Delayed Blitz**: A defender waits a moment before rushing. Strong against play action passes; weak against quick passes.
- **Cross Blitz**: Two rushers cross paths to confuse the blockers. Strong against runs; weak against screens.
- **Free Fire**: Everyone rushes and nobody covers deep. Strong against passes if it gets to the quarterback; weak against screens.
- **Twist Stunt**: Two linemen swap paths as they rush. Strong against passes; weak against draw plays.
- **Loop Stunt**: A lineman loops behind another as he rushes. Strong against passes; weak against draw plays.
- **Edge Blitz**: A defender rushes around the outside. Strong against passes; weak against runs outside.

### Zone Coverages

Defenses where the defenders guard areas of the field.

- **Cover Two Zone**: Two deep defenders each guard half of the far field, and others guard areas near the line. Strong against passes; weak against runs.
- **Cover Three Zone**: Three deep defenders each guard a third of the far field. Strong against passes; weak against quick passes.
- **Underneath Zone**: Defenders guard the short areas to stop short passes. Strong against quick passes; weak against deep passes.
- **Nickel Quick Coverage**: Five defensive backs guard short areas. Strong against quick passes; weak against runs.
- **Tampa Two**: Two deep defenders, with a linebacker dropping back deep up the middle. Strong against deep passes; weak against runs.
- **Cover Three Sky**: Three deep defenders, and one of them also helps against runs. Strong against runs; weak against deep passes.
- **Cover Three Cloud**: Three deep defenders, with a cornerback covering the sideline. Strong against quick passes; weak against deep passes.
- **Cover Three Buzz**: Three deep defenders, and one drops from the middle to cover short crossing receivers. Strong against passes; weak against runs outside.
- **Cover Three Match**: Three deep defenders who follow nearby receivers instead of guarding areas. Strong against deep passes; weak against quick passes.
- **Drop Eight**: Only three rush and eight drop back to cover. Strong against passes; weak against runs.

### Man Coverages

Defenses where each defender covers one receiver.

- **Man Free**: Each defender covers one receiver, with one deep defender helping. Strong against passes; weak against receivers crossing the field.
- **Man Press**: Each defender covers one receiver and bumps him at the line. Strong against quick passes; weak against deep passes.
- **Cover One Robber**: Man for man, with one defender lurking in the middle to jump on short passes. Strong against receivers crossing the field; weak against deep passes.
- **Two Man**: Man for man close to the line, with two deep defenders behind. Strong against passes; weak against quarterback runs.
- **Off Man**: Man for man, but the defenders line up several steps back. Strong against deep passes; weak against quick passes.
- **Man Three Deep**: Only three rush while the rest cover man for man with deep help. Strong against deep passes; weak against runs.
- **Cover One Contain**: Man for man, with a defender keeping the quarterback from running. Strong against quarterback runs; weak against deep passes.

### Two Deep Coverages

Defenses with two or four defenders guarding the far field.

- **Cover Four Quarters**: Four deep defenders each guard a quarter of the far field. Strong against deep passes; weak against quick passes.
- **Cover Six**: A mix: one side of the field has four deep defenders and the other side has two. Strong against deep passes; weak against runs.
- **Palms**: Cornerbacks guard the short area while others guard the far field. Strong against quick passes; weak against deep passes.
- **Match Quarters**: Four deep defenders who change to man for man when receivers cross. Strong against deep passes; weak against receivers crossing the field.
- **Cover Nine**: Man for man on one side and four deep defenders on the other. Strong against deep passes; weak against quick passes.
- **Two Deep Disguise**: It looks like two deep defenders but changes to three at the snap. Strong against passes; weak against runs.
- **Cover Two Match**: Two deep defenders who follow nearby receivers. Strong against deep passes; weak against runs.
- **Cover Two Hard Flat**: Two deep defenders, with the cornerbacks staying close to the sideline. Strong against quick passes; weak against deep passes.
- **Cover Two Invert**: Two deep defenders, with the deep defenders moving closer in. Strong against deep passes; weak against quick passes.
- **Cover Four Drop**: Four deep defenders drop back and others cover short crossing receivers. Strong against receivers crossing the field; weak against quick passes.

### Deep Help

Defenses with extra help against long passes.

- **Deep Thirds**: Three deep defenders guard thirds of the field far from the line. Strong against deep passes; weak against quick passes.
- **Prevent Deep**: Seven defensive backs stay very deep. Strong against deep passes; weak against runs.
- **Double Coverage**: Two defenders guard the best receiver. Strong against deep passes; weak against runs.
- **Dime Short Coverage**: Six defensive backs guard short areas. Strong against quick passes; weak against runs.
- **Dime Deep Coverage**: Six defensive backs, four of them guarding the far field. Strong against deep passes; weak against runs.
- **Bracket Coverage**: Two defenders guard the main receiver, one high and one low. Strong against deep passes; weak against runs.
- **Safety Rotation**: The deep defenders change places after the offense lines up. Strong against deep passes; weak against quick passes.
- **Quarter Defense**: Seven defensive backs in a deep layout. Strong against deep passes; weak against runs.
- **Cover Three Seam**: Three deep defenders, with help in the middle of the field. Strong against deep passes; weak against quick passes.
- **Cover Four Press**: Four deep defenders and cornerbacks bumping at the line. Strong against quick passes; weak against deep passes.

### Situation Defenses

Defenses for special situations, such as the last play of a half.

- **Hail Mary Defense**: Defenders fill the end zone to knock down or catch the desperation pass. Strong against deep passes; weak against runs.
- **Two Point Defense**: A short-field defense for the try after a touchdown, with extra rushers. Strong against passes; weak against runs.
- **Two Minute Defense**: Defenders stay back to prevent a long gain and keep the play in front of them. Strong against deep passes; weak against quick passes.

## Special teams: kicks and punts

### Kick Offs

The kinds of kick that start a half or follow a score.

- **Deep Kick**: The kicker sends the ball far down the field and the other team returns it. Good for distance; a long return is the risk.
- **Short Kick**: A shorter kick that lands before the end zone, so it avoids a touchback. Risky: the other team can return it.
- **Squib Kick**: A low, bouncing kick that is hard to catch. It uses up time and can stop a big return, but the other team starts near midfield.
- **Onside Kick**: A short kick that the kicking team tries to recover itself. It rarely works, so use it only when you are behind late in the game.
- **Safety Punt**: When the other team scores two points by tackling you in your own end zone, you punt the ball from your own twenty yard line. They may return it.

### Punts

The kinds of punt, and the fake punts.

- **Punt**: A kick on fourth down that sends the ball far down the field to the other team. Safe, but it gives up the ball.
- **Pin Punt**: A punt aimed to land near the goal line, so the other team starts far from scoring. If it goes too far, it is a touchback.
- **Rugby Punt**: The kicker runs to the side and kicks on the move, which makes it harder to block. The punt is shorter.
- **Tight Punt**: A punt with everyone close to the center for strong protection against a block. The punt is shorter and easier to return.
- **Fake Punt Run**: The kicker or a back runs with the ball instead of kicking, hoping to gain the yards. Rare, and if it fails the other team gets the ball.
- **Fake Punt Pass**: The kicker throws a pass instead of kicking. Rare: it works if the defense expects a kick, and if it fails the other team gets the ball.

### Field Goals and Tries

Field goals, extra points, and the fake kicks.

- **Field Goal**: A kick through the goal posts worth three points. Choose it on fourth down when you are close enough to kick it.
- **Fake Field Goal Run**: The holder runs with the ball instead of kicking. Rare: if it works the offense keeps the ball, and if it fails the other team takes over.
- **Fake Field Goal Pass**: The holder throws a pass instead of kicking. Rare: it works if the defense expects a kick, and if it fails the other team gets the ball.
- **Extra Point Kick**: A kick through the goal posts after a touchdown, worth one point. Almost always good.
- **Fair Catch Kick**: After a fair catch late in a half, a team may try a field goal from that spot without defenders rushing. Rare.
- **Swinging Gate Try**: A trick on a two point try: the line swings out to one side like a gate and the ball goes to a player who can run or throw. Rare.

### Kick Defense

How your defense plays against a punt or a field goal.

- **Punt Return**: The receiving team catches the punt and runs it back. Good against a long punt; risky if the ball bounces badly.
- **Fair Catch**: The returner signals and catches the ball without being tackled. Safe, but there are no return yards.
- **Punt Block**: The defense rushes to block the kick. A block gives a big gain, but nobody is back to return a punt that is kicked.
- **Punt Safe**: The defense stays back and sets up a return, and does not risk running into the kicker. Safe against a fake.
- **Field Goal Block**: The defense tries to block the field goal. A block saves points; a penalty for hitting the kicker is the risk.
- **Field Goal Safe**: The defense stays back so there is no risk of a penalty, but lets the kick go. Safe against a fake.

## Settings

- **Formation**: Chooses how the quarterback and running backs line up before the snap.
- **Personnel**: Chooses how many running backs, tight ends and receivers are on the field.
- **Motion**: Chooses whether a player runs across the field before the snap.
- **Side**: Chooses whether the play goes to the left or to the right.
- **Tempo**: Chooses how fast the offense gets ready: normal, no huddle, or hurry up, which saves time.
- **Protection**: Chooses how many players stay in to block on a pass.
- **Defensive Personnel**: Chooses how many defensive backs, linebackers and linemen are on the field.
- **Coverage Shade**: Chooses whether the defenders stand close to the receivers or farther back.
