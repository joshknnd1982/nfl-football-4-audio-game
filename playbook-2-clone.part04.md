### 7.22 The music and celebration slots (owner rule 2026-10-24, CLAUDE.md 0.31)

- The original's startup music and its score celebration sounds are not played in the owner's own mode. Each becomes a named slot, read in this order: the player's file of that name in the per-user sounds folder (and numbered extras of it, 7.7.1), the developer's file of that name in the program's own sounds folder (none ships until the owner gives one), else nothing.
- Silence keeps the draws: where the original drew numbers for its music or celebration (which sound, how many, the pitch), the slot draws the same numbers and plays nothing, so a seeded game is the same game; the waits of those sounds are skipped (the moment is quick).
- The startup follows the player's rules setting (the owner's mode by default); a looping music file still ends with its event (0.21). The volume demo plays the music slot's file when there is one, else a short sound of the game that stays.
- The README, the customize help (CUSTOMIZE), the help (F6) and the changelog name the slots and the folders. Tests: no original music or celebration file is played in the owner's mode at the start, a touchdown, the halftime, the championship and the exit; a player's file in each slot is played there; the draws of a seeded game are the same with and without the sounds.

### 7.23 Real rosters, researched in full, built in and kept up to date, in every football game (owner rule 2026-10-25, CLAUDE.md 0.32, phase PR)

- Why: a tester heard a franchise team without its quarterbacks (nfl4, B-P163). The cause was the original's slots used as the roster, and research read through a tool that summarizes pages. Players know the real rosters; they must be right and stay right.
- Research (SR.1, SR.2): read each team's roster and depth chart as raw data (the page's HTML or the data feed it loads) with a script, never through a tool that returns a summary of a page (a summary drops rows and can invent them). Official sources first (the league's and the teams' own pages; college: the schools'), a second reliable source to compare, every disagreement settled by a search or recorded. Keep apart the active roster, the practice squad and the reserve lists (injured reserve and the like). Record per player: name, number, position, depth order, return roles. Record the date, the sources and their terms in the spec (0.19); a source whose terms forbid automated reading is read by hand or not used. Read the terms text and the robots.txt of every candidate source BEFORE choosing it and quote the clause in the spec (nfl4 SR.1: the NFL's terms 1.3 bar systematic retrieval, so the script reads open aggregator data and the official pages are hand-checked samples, B-P166); compare every pull with a few raw official pages by hand, because the sources differ by a few men a team (B-P165). A pull that every player would run is not offered when the source asks for one call a day. Owner decision (nfl4, D-156, 2026-10-25): for these free audio games for blind players the maintainer's script may still read an official site whose terms bar systematic retrieval, gently (robots.txt obeyed, a pause between requests, a User-Agent that names the project, pages kept local and never republished, facts only, never run by the players), the owner carrying the risk and the terms clause recorded in the spec; Claude does not claim the site will not object, and switches the reader off if anyone does.
- Positions: the real positions as the leading commercial game lists them. Football: Madden NFL's roster positions as EA's own ratings pages list them (read as raw HTML in nfl4 SR.1, 2026-10-25, Madden NFL 27: QB, HB, FB, WR, TE, LT, LG, C, RG, RT, LEDG, REDG, DT, SAM, MIKE, WILL, CB, FS, SS, K, P, LS; the older LE, RE, LOLB, MLB, ROLB are gone), with kick and punt returner as depth-chart roles; read the pages again for a new edition, never the list from memory (B-P164). Positions and depth charts are football facts and structure; nothing of EA's code, text, ratings or art is copied. A player keeps his own position in every list, sentence and file: a tight end is never stored as a receiver, a returner never loses his position.
- The model: a team is its roster and its depth chart. The engine's slots (the original's places) are filled from the depth chart for each game and are never the roster. An empty slot never creates a made-up man who takes a roster spot; made-up players exist only where a real team has nobody of a needed position, and in later draft classes. The original's exact mode keeps the original's slots and rosters (golden transcript).
- The data file: customizable (7.9), a VERSION line with the date, healed when damaged (7.10), a player's lines for a team replace that team's lines.
- Lists and lags (nfl4 SR.2, B-P167, B-P168): read on a game day, the teams' pages list the day's elevated practice squad men as active (more than 53): send back to the squad every active man the last published file had on that team's squad, at most 2. Every source lags, the official pages too: the script sorts the differences by kind, a search settles the rest, and each answer goes into a settlements file with its source, applied by every later run. Take ages from birth dates (B-P169); keep roles such as nickel back and returner apart from positions (B-P170).
- The update script `game/tools/update_rosters.py`: pulls every team, checks (the league's roster limits per team, the positions every team must have such as a quarterback, a kicker, a punter and a long snapper, every name once, every position known, the second source agrees on who is where), writes the data file and a report of every change since the last file (signings, releases, trades, moves to a reserve list), and never replaces the file when a check fails. Its tests use saved pages, never the network. The maintainer reviews the report and publishes the checked file in the repository (a `rosters` folder).
- The in-game update: an item (football: F9, Customize, and the franchise menu) that, only when the player asks, downloads the newest checked file from the repository, checks it, keeps the old file as a backup and says in one sentence the date it now has, that it already had the newest, or why it could not. A second item may run the pull itself with the same checks. Keys per 0.24, a description per 0.25. Nothing is downloaded by itself (0.15).
- Saved games: a season, league or franchise saved with an older or smaller roster is converted when opened (real positions back by name, the depth charts built, missing real men added from the file when no other team has them), the old file kept as a backup, one sentence said. A franchise in progress keeps its own history and is offered once to bring the other teams up to the current rosters.
- Tests: every team of a new game, season and franchise equals the file (names, positions, numbers, order; nobody left off, no made-up man); old saves converted; the script's checks pass on the shipped file and fail on damaged copies; the in-game update with a fake server (newer, same, damaged, no connection); the long soaks and their bands; the original's mode unchanged.
- The roster as a depth chart (nfl4 SR.5, B-P177 to B-P183): when the engine plays fixed slots (the original's places), store only the chart (every man of the roster once at his own position, each position in the staff's order, the player's own order kept) and make the slots from it in memory after every change; every block that moves a man (cut, draft, signings, trades, injuries, retirements, squads) does it through one join and one leave; a save makes every view again and logs what it had to mend. The cut keeps the roster limit by the family minimums first, then value; needs are a family under the real league's mean (rounded down) or a weak starter; made-up men take real last names (rule 0.19, B-P182).
- The roster heard and chosen by position (nfl4 SR.6, B-W23, B-P184 to B-P186): the roster is one list with a heading and a count for each real position and then the squad and the reserve lists; each man is said by his own position, his depth, his number and, when he returns kicks, "kick returner"; Left and Right go position by position, a letter to a position, never the timeout key. One lineup item lists every starter and one choice list at each position (the men of the position, the men of his family who can move there, the squad's men; a squad man is an elevation, two a game, or a signing in a franchise); the same questions serve a quick game and a franchise (two backends); a starter the player chose who leaves the team is told once, with the man who starts now. Returners are roles laid over the two return places at the game's door, not stored places. A name two men share is told apart by position and team in lists of several teams.
