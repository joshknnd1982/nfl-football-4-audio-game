## 0. Core rules, continued (part 3)

This part holds the rules after 0.40 (`AGENTS.part02.md` is full). Every session reads it in full, after `AGENTS.part02.md`. Sections 1 to 4 and 10 stay in `AGENTS.part02.md`.

0.41 Open questions that a player can answer are asked on GitHub (owner rule, 2026-11-15, every game, program and utility that has a GitHub repository; nfl4 first). The owner's words: "put open questions in the github comments so Dennis and others can answer them. make this a rule in one of the global markdown files for all games." So in every program:
(1) Which questions. Every open question whose answer a player or tester can give goes to GitHub: what he meant in a report, what he heard, how he wants a feature to behave, which of two or more designs he prefers, whether a fix does what he wanted, and every choice the agent left open for the tester (a "would show it wrong" or "if the tester wants" in a decision, a state file or `NEXT_PROMPT.txt`). Such a question is never left only in a file of the folder. What only the owner can answer stays with the owner (rule 0.8: sign-ins, money, releases and other public choices, legal matters, his own wishes as the game's maker, his listening); what the agent can find by reading, running or searching stays with the agent (0.8). When players disagree, the owner decides.
(2) Where. On the issue the question belongs to (the report or request it came from), as a comment; a closed issue too, since its author still gets the comment. A question that belongs to no issue goes on a new issue titled "Open question: <the topic in a few words>" with the label `question` (created when the repository lacks it). Issues and comments are public: they hold no private data (the limits of rule 0.15: no account name, e-mail address, password, key or token; a problem report is quoted only as far as the question needs), and they never use `@` before a name (the issue's author is already told).
(3) How. In the session where the question arises, or the first session that finds it unasked: one comment per issue, headed "Open questions", the questions numbered, at most five, each in plain words for a player who has not read the code: what the game does now, the choices with the present behaviour or the recommended one first, and why the answer matters; the comment ends with how to answer ("reply here with the number of the question and your choice; anyone who plays the game may answer") and says that nothing waits on the answer, the game keeps what it does now. A question already asked and still unanswered is not asked again; a later reply on that issue may remind once.
(4) Recorded. `spec/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` gets the item (3.2.1) with the status `asked-on-github`, the comment's address, the date and the default the game keeps until it is answered (the default is a D entry when it is a design choice), so no work waits for it.
(5) Read and settled. Every session first reads the new comments on the repository's open issues, and on issues where a question is asked (`gh issue view <n> --comments`, or the issue list sorted by update); an answer settles its item (status answered, who answered, the comment's address, the tag SRC-PLAYER), and an answer that asks for work is a tester's request like any other (built, tested, logged, in the CHANGELOG, at the owner's word where 0.8 or 0.13 say so). The session's report and `NEXT_PROMPT.txt` name the questions asked, their addresses and the answers read.
(6) Checked. The end-of-session sweep of 3.2.1 lists every item with status `asked-on-github` and every choice left open for a tester in the session's decisions; an open choice for a tester with no comment address is a fault to fix before the session ends.
Checklist item A133; entry B-T159 in `fixesforgames`. nfl4: Q-084 onward in its `spec/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

0.42 A T pressed during a live play is not kept, except in the last two minutes of a half (owner rule, 2026-11-15, every American football game of the owner's of any kind, NFL, college or any other league; nfl4 first; it amends 0.22). A tester of nfl4 answered on GitHub (issue 11, rule 0.41): "A with an acception. You wouldn't want a timeout most likely after the play is over", and for the last two minutes of each half chose that a T during the play still calls the time out when it ends; the owner, asked whether this changes for every football game: "Yes, all football games". So in every such game:
(1) T before the snap or the kick works as 0.22 says (the play is withdrawn, the time out called at once); the ball is live from the snap or the kick (the quarterback's "Hike!", the holder's or punter's "hike").
(2) T pressed while the ball is live is NOT kept: no time out is charged, the clock after the play runs exactly as it would without the key (the same random draws), and when the play ends one short sentence says that the time out came after the snap and none was taken; where a question waits while the ball is live (a run-out question), the sentence is said at once and not again.
(3) The exception: in the last two minutes of a half (the second and the fourth quarter with 2:00 or less, the time of the two-minute warning, or the league's equivalent), T pressed while the ball is live is kept and the time out is called the moment the play ends, before anything else (the old words of 0.22), so the seconds before the next snap are saved; the waiting question says so instead.
(4) Overtime and play-off periods are decided per game and recorded (a D entry), with the choice asked on GitHub when a tester may care (nfl4: Q-098, default not kept).
(5) A T kept while the ball is not in play (a pause between periods) is still granted at the next menu. The key help, F6 and the README say all of it in T's line (0.24); the log line `timeout_key` says late, or kept with its reason (0.15); tests cover the window's edge (2:00 kept, 2:01 not), the quarters outside it, overtime, the question's sentence and the equal draws; the CHANGELOG item says how to test (0.29).
Playbook 7.16; checklist item A76; entries B-W49, B-W50. nfl4: D-233, D-242.
