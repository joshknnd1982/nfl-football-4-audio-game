### B3. Contracts and the salary cap

Reason: contract structure is a table-and-slider screen, and EA itself shipped bugs in it (void years counted wrong, section G).

After the beta EA let the player set years, salary and bonus before the talk starts [launch guide 2026-07-31, SRC-OFFICIAL]. VERDICT: ADOPT the shape, not the meters: "He asks 12.4 million for 4 years. 1 accept, 2 offer less, 3 offer more years, 4 let him go", at most 3 rounds, with the outcome depending on his mood. Size: part of F6. Reason: a few spoken lines, a real decision.
- Franchise tag and transition tag. NFL rules [SRC-OFFICIAL, operations.nfl.com franchise-tags]: one tag per team per year; tag window 2027 league year: 2027-02-17 to 4 p.m. 2027-03-03; non-exclusive tag pays the greater of the five-year cap-percentage average at the position or 120 percent of the player's prior-year salary, other teams may make an offer, original team may match within 5 days or take two first-round picks; exclusive tag pays the greater of the top five salaries at the position this year or the non-exclusive amount and bars other teams; transition tag is the top-10 average (or 120 percent) with a right to match and no compensation; extension deadline July 15. VERDICT: ADOPT one franchise tag per offseason (exclusive style), pay = average of the five highest salaries at his position slot in the league; LATER non-exclusive; SKIP transition tag. Size: small, part of F6.
- Restricted free agent tenders (first-round, second-round, original-round, right-of-first-refusal) [Madden 27 and NFL 2026 values, SRC-OFFICIAL: 8.107, 5.811, 3.674, 3.547 million dollars]. VERDICT: SKIP. Reason: removes a whole menu.
- Rookie contracts: drafted players 4 years, undrafted 3, first-round picks have a fifth-year option [NFL, SRC-OFFICIAL, operations.nfl.com contract-language]. VERDICT: ADOPT 4 years at a fixed pay scale by pick number (the NFL's exact wage scale was not found, UNVERIFIED; the game invents its own scale between the minimum salary and about 15 percent of the cap); SKIP the fifth-year option. Size: small.
- Restructure, signing-bonus proration, dead money. NFL: dead money means money already committed that is charged to the cap if a player is cut; proration is spread up to 5 seasons [SRC-OFFICIAL]. SKIP restructures and proration. Reason: the biggest cause of broken computer-team cap in Madden.
- Cap hold, cap chart. Added to Madden 27 after the beta [launch guide, SRC-OFFICIAL]. VERDICT: SKIP; the cap space number is enough.

### B4. Free agency

- Free Agent Frenzy (EA's name): all 32 teams bid in a compressed window; EA says 12 hours, advanced hour by hour (up to 12 rounds, set by the commissioner); offers are judged together when the round advances, so first to click no longer wins; interested teams are visible; offers can be withdrawn [Deep Dive, launch guide, SRC-OFFICIAL]. After the 2026-09-16 update players weigh fit and total value, the top-rated players are judged first, and computer teams chase several players at a position of need, re-check their need every round, and stop when the need is filled [SRC-OFFICIAL]. VERDICT: ADOPT as "free agency days": 3 to 5 rounds; each round the player hears the market in short pages, places up to a few offers, then hears the results. Size: large with F6. Reason: round-based, discrete, perfect for listening; the evaluation rule (best fit and value, best player first) is a fair rule to copy.
- Free agent categories and timing, NFL: unrestricted (4 or more accrued seasons), restricted (3), exclusive rights (fewer than 3); a season accrues with at least six regular-season games on full pay; minimum salary by credited seasons for 2026: 0 seasons 885,000; 1 season 1,005,000; 2 seasons 1,075,000; 3 seasons 1,145,000; 4 to 6 seasons 1,215,000; 7 or more 1,300,000 dollars [SRC-OFFICIAL, operations.nfl.com types-of-free-agents, retrieved 2026-10-14]. VERDICT: ADOPT unrestricted free agency only, with the minimum-salary table as data. Size: part of F6.
- Undrafted rookie free agents window after the draft [Madden 27, SRC-OFFICIAL]. VERDICT: LATER (small). Use it first as the automatic way to fill empty slots.
- Compensatory picks for lost free agents [NFL: as many as 32 formula picks at the end of rounds 3 to 7, none to a club for more than four; 2026: 33 awarded to 15 clubs (32 formula + 1 special), SRC-OFFICIAL, media.nfl.com 2026-03-09]. VERDICT: SKIP at first; LATER as a simple rule (a club that lost more free agents than it gained gets an extra pick in rounds 5 to 7).

### B5. Trades

The computer answers by a value rule (value from rating, age, potential and salary; picks valued by round; rebuilding teams value picks and youth more, contenders value rating more). Computer-to-computer trades: LATER and only a few per year. Size: medium (F7). Reason: one question, one answer, but the value rule needs a long test.

The 2026-09-16 update lowered retirement of high-rated quarterbacks in their early thirties and of running backs early in their careers [SRC-OFFICIAL]. VERDICT: ADOPT (core). ### B9. Owner, job security, goals, approval

- Approval Rating (Madden 26): measures how five groups see the head coach: the general manager, coaching staff, players, fans and media; opinions shift weekly with results; at the season's end coaches (user and computer) are judged on it and a low rating with weak support means being fired; a rating carries over; narrative goals from "storylines" also move it [Madden 26 deep dive, SRC-OFFICIAL]. Madden 27's tips page tells players not to ignore it, and the 2026-09-16 update fixed a user coach being fired after a winning season that ended with a play-off loss, and a fired coach receiving an offer from the team that had just fired him [SRC-OFFICIAL]. An owner with a budget and goals: the search results mention one in a Madden 26 guide only (SRC-SECONDARY, not on an EA page I read; UNVERIFIED for Madden 27). VERDICT: ADOPT one approval number 0 to 100 and one season goal from the owner ("win at least 9 games", "reach the play-offs") with a spoken verdict at the season's end; being fired is possible, never after a winning season; after firing the player is offered 3 teams. Size: medium (F9). Reason: this is the cheapest source of long-term tension: one number, one goal, one verdict.
- Season, game and career goals that give XP; weekly in-game goals [SRC-OFFICIAL]. VERDICT: LATER (one weekly "game goal" spoken before kick-off). Reason: nice but the owner goal does the job first.

VERDICT: SKIP the 65 types; LATER a small version: 4 or 5 personality words (for example money, winning, loyalty, playing time) set at birth, each with one rule in contract talks and morale. Size: medium. Reason: this is EA's main new idea and gives drama, but 65 types are not buildable or listenable; five are.
- Holdouts (a player stays away when he thinks he is underpaid or unhappy; you can negotiate, talk, wait, or trade him) and trade demands (the step after a holdout; it becomes news, other teams make offers) [SRC-OFFICIAL]. Conversations with unhappy players to change his mindset [SRC-OFFICIAL]. VERDICT: LATER: one spoken event at the start of camp, "Ruiz holds out. 1 pay him, 2 wait, 3 trade him". Reason: a high-value, short, audio-friendly event, but it needs personality and contracts first.
- Morale: players with a breakout game get a bigger morale boost if young or lower rated [Madden 26 deep dive, SRC-OFFICIAL]; Madden 27 tips tell the player to "monitor morale" [SRC-OFFICIAL]. VERDICT: LATER (one number per player, up or down by results and playing time, moving the rating by at most 1 or 2 points through H8).
- Emergent Actions (EA's name): the week's decisions, each asking for a real choice, surfaced from what happens in the league (a player wants a bigger role, a contract is heating up, a roster question before game day); staff can handle them for you, and you see the plan first; computer teams use the same system [Deep Dive, Franchise Hub guide: "Action Cards", SRC-OFFICIAL]. VERDICT: ADOPT as a "to-do list": before each game a short numbered list of 0 to 3 items with the staff's recommendation first ("1 do it, 2 do something else, 3 later"); an "auto" setting accepts every recommendation. Size: small to medium (F5). Reason: this is the one idea that makes Franchise short to listen to. Do not call it "Emergent Actions" or "Action Cards".

## C. What the original already gives Franchise (from GAME_SPEC 1.2, read only for sizing)

- 32 teams in 8 divisions of 4; the player's team has 6 running backs, 6 receivers, 17 defensive players, 2 special teams players, 2 kickers (33 names) and a quarterback; the opponent gets new names each game (5.5). The surname pool has 1,708 names.
- Computer games and play-off games without the player are decided by a score table and a coin flip (5.14, 5.17): team strength has no effect today. Franchise must change this through bounded modifiers (N6.3).
- The play engine draws the ball carrier by slot (5.5, L408); with H7 and H8 a rating can weight that draw.
- No per-player statistics exist for computer games, so player awards (MVP, leaders) have no data; the plan above avoids needing them.
- Position labels for the 17 defensive slots are not given in the original. SX.8 must give each slot a position label (for example line, linebacker, back) before a draft or a position-based aging curve can work. This is a dependency of F1 and F3.

## F. How EA's computer teams manage themselves, and the NFL numbers

### F1. Computer teams in the offseason (in my own words)

EA does not publish one document about it; the picture below is assembled from EA's Madden 26 and 27 pages and update notes (SRC-OFFICIAL). Computer teams play by the same systems as the user: they receive the same weekly decisions, can delegate to staff, hire and fire coaches, and lose coordinators who are hired away as head coaches. A coach on any team is judged on approval at the end of the season and can be fired. In free agency each team has needs by position, and each round it bids for several players at positions where it is short, judges the best players first, rechecks its needs and stops when a need is filled, without stacking signings at one position and without taking on big dead money or going far over next year's cap. Offers are judged by fit and total value, so the top offer should win. Before the draft, teams trade picks and players depending on whether they are contending or rebuilding, how much the player helps now and what he costs; during the draft they pick by team need and best prospect, with real-life habits (more linemen, taking several players at one position, valuing running backs more again). Players are tagged by age and role (rising, veteran, on the trade block) and retire by age and rating, with aging that depends on position. After the update of 2026-09-16 computer teams also restructure big contracts less often.
Design rule for the game, from this: every computer team does the same five things each offseason in a fixed order (retirements, re-sign its own best players by value, free agency in rounds, draft by need, fill empty slots), always ends with every slot filled and under the cap, and one hard invariant test (section G) checks it for 20 seasons.

## G. Design lessons from EA's own list of problems (all SRC-OFFICIAL, EA title-update notes 2026-08-25 and 2026-09-16)

EA's Franchise team publicly lists these problems in Madden 27 after launch. Each one maps to a test the game's 20-season simulation must pass (N6 "done when").
- Players retiring at illogical points: test that no player under 30 retires, and that retirement chance rises with age and falls with rating.
- Free agents and re-signed players asking for unrealistic long deals: test a maximum contract length by age.
- The best offer losing the player: test that the player with the highest value offer wins when two teams bid and both can afford it.
- Negotiation options that force extra years or inflated bonuses: keep negotiation choices independent.
- Computer teams with huge cap deficits that never recover; computer teams restructuring large deals every year; computer teams taking on big dead money: test that every team is under the cap after every offseason, and that no team's cap space is negative at the start of any season.
- Computer teams with no depth at some positions: test that every slot of every team is filled after the offseason fill, and that no position group falls under a fixed minimum rating.
- Computer draft trades happening only if a human simulates the draft: do all computer picks and trades in one code path, never dependent on how the player watched.
- Delegation re-signing a player after the setting said no: test the "auto" setting off means nothing is decided for the player.
- A user head coach fired after a winning season; a fired coach getting an offer from the team that fired him: the game's rule: never fired after a winning season, offers only from other teams.
- Void years counted wrong when a contract ends: not used (contract structure is SKIP), but the same family of bug (a charge counted in the wrong year) applies to dead money: test the cap sum by year.
- Cap value inflating by a compounding multiplier: test the cap and salary scale across 20 seasons, no drift beyond a fixed growth rule.
