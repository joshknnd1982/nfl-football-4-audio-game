## 3. Cross-cutting requirements

- C1 Switches. Under "Rules" in the F9 menu: one on/off per feature NX1 to NX4 and NX7, the playbook choice (NX5), and "Franchise" as a game mode chosen when a new season starts (NX6). Settings are kept in the settings file. A saved game remembers its own mode and state: football.sav gets a marked extra section in the manner of D-027 item 3 (a version value, then the extras' values); a classic save loads in any mode with the extras at their start values; an extras save never loads wrongly into Classic (it asks the player once, spoken).
- C2 The clock. The original has no clock: a quarter is a number of counted plays and F4 invents a time (GAME_SPEC 5.3, 5.6). Timeouts, the two-minute warning, challenge limits, onside kicks late in the game and penalty runoffs need real time. In N. F. L. rules mode a quarter is 15 minutes of game clock. Every play costs seconds by the NFL clock rules (the clock stops on an incomplete pass, a score, a change of possession, a runner out of bounds (restart on the ready signal, on the snap in the last 2:00 of the first half and the last 5:00 of the second), a penalty, a timeout and the two-minute warning; it keeps running after an in-bounds run or catch, a first down and a sack) [SRC-OFFICIAL, Rule 4, 4-3 and 4-4; NFL_RULES_RESEARCH Part 2 section 4], and F4 speaks the real time. The model is design section 5. Calibration: over 10,000 simulated quarters the average number of counted plays per quarter stays within the original's range (29 to 36 by 5.6 item 5) and each quarter ends when the clock reaches zero. The original's count clock remains for Classic. The computer's choices that read "plays left in the period" (5.7 items a and c) read "time left" in N. F. L. rules mode through the same hook (H1). Accepted default (NQ3); the owner may veto.
- C3 Neutral equals classic. Where a feature changes probabilities (ratings, playbooks, penalties' frequency), the neutral setting (all ratings average, playbook Classic, penalties off) gives exactly the original's tables of 5.7 to 5.10.
- C4 Random draws. All new draws use the one `GameRandom` with its bounded redraws (G-D1); each new draw is documented like the original's (range, order, redraw rule) in the design file; tests use the strict scripted source. In Classic mode the extras consume no random number at all.
- C5 Speech texts. New spoken sentences are listed with an ID and exact spelling in `spec\STRINGS_NX.md` (written by SX.1; a generator, never typed by hand, D-017). Pools of remarks (penalty announcements, review announcements, timeout remarks, onside remarks, keeper remarks, the franchise's news lines) are customizable commentary under CLAUDE.md 0.12 and playbook 7.9: permanent IDs, the player can change, switch off or add lines of own text or own sounds, with the file unchanged the defaults speak as written. The inventory is added to `spec\CUSTOMIZABLE_CONTENT.md` as section 13 (SX.1).
- C6 Help. F5, F6 and F7 (special keys, game instructions, football terminology and hints) are the original's strings. In N. F. L. rules mode each gets one added, separate section after the original text (written with the feature, listed in STRINGS_NX.md); the original text is never changed. Penalty names, timeout, challenge, onside kick, keeper, playbook and the franchise terms are explained in the F7 addition.
- C7 Statistics. The quarter statistics (4.19) keep their sentences. In N. F. L. rules mode, added lines (spoken after the original ones): penalties and penalty yards of each side, timeouts used, challenges won and lost, onside kicks tried and recovered, the quarterback's runs and yards (also counted in running plays and running yards, as in the NFL).
- C8 Tests (CLAUDE.md 1: run before saying it works). Each feature has: rule tests against `nfl_rules_<year>` (the numbers), scripted-random flow tests with the exact spoken order, property tests of the NFL invariants (never more than 3 charged timeouts per half, 2 per regular-season overtime period, 3 per pair of play-off overtime periods; never more than 3 challenges per game and a third only after a success; a distance penalty is never enforced beyond half the distance to the goal, except the spot fouls, intentional grounding at the spot of the pass, palpably unfair acts and a safety by enforcement; and so on) [SRC-OFFICIAL, 4-5-1, 16-3-e, 16-4-g, 15-1-1, 14-2-1], and a Classic golden test: a full seeded game with the extras code present and switched off gives the same spoken and sound transcript and the same sequence of random draws as before the extras existed (the golden file is recorded by S7.3b and S7.3c, before any extra is built).
- C9 Accessibility. Every decision point speaks what is being asked, the choices, and the current answer; Up and Down or Tab change it, a letter key sets it, Enter takes it, F1 repeats the situation (position, down, clock, timeouts) as now; a wrong key is echoed as in the original; nothing happens on a timer without a spoken warning; no prompt waits for sight. The accept-or-decline prompt and the challenge prompt speak the result of each choice before the answer is taken (NX7, NX2).
- C10 Robustness. A damaged or missing extras file is repaired from the defaults with a spoken notice (playbook 7.10); the extras never end the program; Save Game works at every wait where it works now and, in N. F. L. rules mode, also while a decision is pending (the pending decision is saved).

## 5. Hooks the classic engine must leave (sessions S7.3b, S7.3c, S7.4, S7.5, S7.7)

The classic engine is built first and exact. So that the extras do not force a rewrite, these seams are built into it, with no change to what Classic does (the golden file of C8 proves it). Each hook is a plain function or data list that Classic fills with the original's behavior.

- H1 One period-clock interface: "plays left", "last play of the period", "end of the period", "two-minute warning due", "speak the clock". Classic answers from the play counter (5.3, 5.6); N. F. L. rules mode answers from seconds. Every place of 5.6 and 5.7 that reads the counter reads it through this interface.
- H2 A play is three steps: choose (menus and 5.7), resolve (5.8 to 5.10 produce one `PlayOutcome` record: yards, kind, spoken sentences and sounds in order, turnover, score, flags) and apply (5.11 and 5.12: position, downs, possession, score). Between resolve and apply there is one hook, `after_outcome(outcome)`, where penalties and challenges may change or hold the outcome. Classic returns it unchanged. The original's order of speech and sound inside a play is kept by the record.
- H3 The offense menu and the defense menu are data lists (item text, key, action), built by one function that the extras (more items, a playbook) can replace. Classic is the exact lists of 4.15, including Save Game's number.
- H4 The kick-off is one function with a `kind` argument (Classic: normal) and the return or recovery step replaceable (onside).
- H5 The game state is one record with an `extras` slot that is empty in Classic; Save Game and loading write and read that slot after the original's values (C1).
- H6 One `Rules` object (Classic = all off) is passed through the engine; the engine calls hook functions that Classic leaves as no-ops, and never tests feature flags itself in the middle of 5.7 to 5.12.
- H7 Rosters stay slot-based (the 33 names of 5.5 and the quarterback) with an empty per-slot `rating` that Classic never reads (franchise, NX6).
- H8 The outcome tables of 5.9 and 5.10 are data (minimum, maximum, sack percent, completion percent, interception rule, fumble chance), read through one `modifiers` argument that Classic passes as neutral (playbooks, ratings, QB keepers).
- H9 Speech and pause helpers (say to say6, dy, rady) are used as they are; extras add only new lines through them.

## 7. NX2 Replay challenges (following NFL challenge rules)

Rule outline (checked by SX.0; corrected in 0.2):
- Each team has two challenges per game; each needs a timeout in hand; a lost challenge is charged a timeout, a won one costs nothing; at least ONE success earns a third challenge; a fourth is never allowed. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 15-1-1(d) to (f), the 2024 amendment in force in 2026]
- No coach's challenge of a play that BEGINS after the two-minute warning of each half (the last play before the warning can still be challenged), in overtime, of scoring plays, tries and turnovers (booth only), by a team whose foul prevents the next snap, or by a team with no challenge or no timeout left. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 15-1-1, 15-1-2, 16-5-d]
- The replay official "may initiate" a review of scores, tries, interceptions, fumbles recovered by the opponent, fourth-down failures, every play after the two-minute warning and every overtime play, with no timeout charged and no cap. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 15-2; in practice every score and turnover is checked, the league page says so]
- A challenge when not allowed costs a timeout; with none left it is a 15-yard foul enforced between downs. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 14-1-2, 14-1-3. The clone offers the challenge only while it is allowed, so this cannot arise; design 6.4.]
- Reviewable: catch or no catch, fumble or down by contact, out of bounds, the goal line, the line to gain, the number of players, the game clock; penalty administration (spot, down, yards). Not reviewable: judgment fouls including pass interference, intentional grounding, ineligible receiver downfield, illegal contact, illegal block, an erroneous whistle. Standard: clear and obvious visual evidence. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 15-2-1, 15-4, 15-7]
- 2026 changes: none to counts, timeouts or reviewable plays; the head coach's pregame designee may throw the flag. [SRC-OFFICIAL for the book; SRC-SECONDARY for the summer edit]

Requirements:
- N2.1 The ruling model. The simulation has no video, so some plays get an initial "ruling on the field" that is wrong with a small, tunable probability by category (a completion ruled incomplete or the reverse, a runner ruled down before a fumble, a runner ruled short of or over the goal line, a catch ruled in or out of bounds). The frequency, categories and the chance that the replay shows the error as clear and obvious are in a data table with their sources (SX.0 and the design session set them from published NFL replay statistics and record how). A wrong ruling is not hidden: it is the thing that a challenge or a booth review can reverse.
- N2.2 The player hears the ruling, a short color remark from the announcer that hints at how close it looked (in the manner of Ghip's remarks; customizable pool), and has the challenge key (the letter C and an added menu item) only while a challenge is allowed by the rules (the rule outline above). The prompt speaks the cost ("you have <n> challenges and <n> timeouts left; a timeout is lost if the ruling stands", STRINGS_NX NXR-02).
- N2.3 The review: the announcer says the referee is going under the hood (a pause drawn in the original's manner with dy), then "the ruling on the field is confirmed" or "reversed" with the new result applied through H2. The outcome and the next sentences follow from the reversed result as they would from the play had it been called right.
- N2.4 Booth reviews (scoring plays, turnovers, the last two minutes, overtime): spoken as "the replay official has stopped the game to review"; no cost; the same reversal logic.
- N2.5 The computer coach challenges by a rule of thumb (design 6.4: a call against it, the hint it heard, a challenge and a timeout in hand; calibrated to the NFL's 0.24 to 0.28 challenges per team per game, 38 to 52 percent reversed, NFL_RULES_RESEARCH Part 1.6), sometimes wrongly, so it can lose a timeout and a challenge.
- N2.7 After a reversal a head coach may change an accept or decline choice made on that play (15-7-3 Item 3); the player is asked again (NX7).
- N2.6 A challenge never reaches the original's F2 "instant replay" (the replay of the speech lines of the last play stays as it is and is named in the menus only as instant replay).

## 8. NX3 QB keepers (according to NFL rules)

Reading (NQ1): designed quarterback runs and the NFL's treatment of a quarterback who runs.

Rule outline (checked by SX.0; corrected in 0.2):
- A quarterback who holds a live ball is a runner (3-27); his rushes and yards count as rushing, unless the play is a sack, which needs an apparent attempt to pass; a designed keeper stopped behind the line is a rush for a loss. Passer protections follow his "passing posture" and end when he becomes a runner; outside the pocket area he loses part of them. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 3-27, 12-2-9, 12-2-11; Guide for Statisticians pp. 11, 14 to 15]
- A runner who slides or dives feet or head first, or fakes it, is down the instant he touches the ground; defenders must pull up; unavoidable contact by a defender already committed is not a foul unless it is a forcible hit to the head or neck or other unnecessary roughness; late contact on a slid runner is unnecessary roughness, 15 yards, automatic first down. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 7-2-1(c) and (d), 12-2-8(d)]
- Intentional grounding: loss of down and 10 yards from the previous spot, or loss of down at the spot of the pass when that is worse, or a safety in the end zone; a passer outside the "pocket area" (the book's term, not "tackle box") who throws to or beyond the line of scrimmage is not grounding. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 8-2-1, 3-25]
- The quarterback sneak and the push (the "tush push") are legal in 2026: 12-1-4 bans pulling the runner, not pushing him; the 2025 ban vote failed 22 to 10 (24 needed); no ban was proposed in 2026. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 12-1-4; nfl.com]
- A fumble by a runner on fourth down, after the two-minute warning or on a try may be advanced only by the fumbler. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 8-7-5, 8-7-6; the clone has no recovery by the offense, so it changes no result]
- Fumble and injury exposure of a running quarterback (the original has no injuries). Injuries are built in the first release by SX.8k but ONLY inside a Franchise (D-062, INJ-1): a quarterback's run in a quick game or a Classic season carries no injury risk; in a franchise game it is one of the plays that can hurt its runner (INJ-3).

Requirements:
- N3.1 New offense plays, each a named play with an outcome row against the six defense calls (data, H8): QB Sneak (short yardage, high success, tiny fumble chance, the right play on third and fourth and short), QB Keeper (a bootleg or naked keeper to the outside, medium yards, a chance of a loss that counts as a rush for a loss, not a sack), QB Draw (a pass look that becomes a run, strong against a blitz, weak against a short run defense), and the Scramble (a called pass play that, instead of the incompletion or the sack, ends with the quarterback running; it extends the original's drop-back sentences "scrambles left looking for a receiver" and "is forced out of the pocket", which today lead to ordinary pass results). The tables are SX.1's design (NFL_EXTRAS_DESIGN 7.1, start values), calibrated in SX.5 from NFL quarterback rushing statistics (NFL_RULES_RESEARCH Part 1.6 and Part 9 section 2: designed runs 3.66 yards, scrambles 7.67, third or fourth and 1 converted 83.4 percent).
- N3.2 The slide: when a keeper gains a first down or is near the sideline or the goal line, a slide may be chosen by the quarterback (an automatic rule for the computer; for the player a quarterback who runs the play slides when the situation says so, announced, never a prompt in the middle of a play).
- N3.3 Statistics: the keeper counts as a running play and its yards as running yards, plus its own line in the added statistics (C7); a fumble counts as a fumble lost when lost.
- N3.4 Sentences in the manner of 5.9 ("<quarterback> keeps the ball and runs around the right end", "the quarterback sneak is good for a first down"), a new phrase family with the original's "never repeat one of the last N" rule, customizable (C5).
- N3.5 The computer chooses keepers (short-yardage sneaks, late-game scrambles when trailing, rarely otherwise) by rules in its design file; the defense calls against a keeper follow 5.7's matching logic extended to the new plays.
- Done when: the table values and the rule tests pass, every slide case is tested, a seeded long run's statistics are inside the NFL ranges the design file names, and the Classic golden test passes.

## 12. NX7 Penalties (accept or decline, according to NFL rules)

Rule outline (checked by SX.0, NFL_RULES_RESEARCH Parts 6 and 7; corrected in 0.2; the list is data and can grow):
- The offended team may decline any penalty unless a rule forbids it, and play goes on as if there were no foul; it may also accept the penalty and decline only its yardage. One captain indicates the choice after the Referee explains it (the rulebook names no coach; the clone lets the player, as head coach, decide). The choice is final once the ball is ready, except that a head coach may change it after a replay reversal. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 14-1-1, 18-1-3, 15-7-3]
- Automatic first down is the default for every foul by the defense on a scrimmage down, except ten listed fouls (offside, encroachment, neutral zone infraction, delay of game, illegal substitution, excess timeout, running into the kicker, more than 11 in the formation or at the snap, defensive illegal formation on a scrimmage kick). Offense fouls keep the down unless they carry a loss of down (the combination penalties: illegal forward pass, intentional grounding and a few others); an enforcement that reaches the line to gain is first and 10. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 14-1-2 Items 2, 4, 5]
- Spots: previous, succeeding, spot of the foul, dead ball, basic; runs by "three-and-one"; passes from the previous spot except pass interference and personal fouls; the half-distance rule with its exceptions (spot fouls, intentional grounding at the spot of the pass, palpably unfair acts, safety by enforcement). One penalty per team per down, chosen by the offended team; double fouls offset and the down is replayed. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 3-13-3, 14-2-1, 14-3, 14-4, 14-5-1]
- Dead and live: false start, encroachment and neutral zone infraction are whistled dead before the snap; offside, illegal formation, motion and shift are called at the snap with the play run (the 0.1 word "pre-snap" covered both). [SRC-OFFICIAL, 7-4, 14-4-1, 14-4-9]
- Clock: after a penalty the clock starts as if there had been no foul, but on the snap after the first-half warning, in the last five minutes of the SECOND HALF (not "the fourth quarter and overtime" as 0.1 said), for an offense foul in the fourth period or regular-season overtime after the ball was ready, and after a second delay; the 10-second runoff exists only after the two-minute warning of either half; there is no "inside one minute" rule. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 4-3-2(e), 4-7]
- Scores: a defense personal or unsportsmanlike foul on a touchdown play is enforced on the try or the kick-off; an accepted offense foul erases a score; an accepted defensive foul on the last play of a half gives one untimed down. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 14-2-3, 4-8-2(a)]
- The list to start from (design 9.2, 27 rows with the 2025 frequency mix): false start, delay of game, offside, neutral zone infraction, encroachment, illegal formation, illegal shift, offensive and defensive holding, illegal use of hands, illegal contact, pass interference (both), roughing the passer, intentional grounding, ineligible receiver downfield, face mask, unnecessary roughness, illegal block in the back, horse collar, unsportsmanlike conduct, running into and roughing the kicker, kick-off offside, holding on a return, too many men on the field, illegal touching of an onside kick. [SRC-OFFICIAL for the yards and spots; the frequencies SRC-SECONDARY, Part 8]

Requirements:
- N7.1 The foul table is data, with a permanent ID per foul: name, spoken text, who commits it, the phases it can happen in (before the snap, run, pass, kick), yards, automatic first down, loss of down, enforcement spot, whether the down is replayed, and its base chance per play type. Frequencies are calibrated to NFL accepted-penalty averages per game and per team (SX.0 records the sources and the average per play), with the quarter's feel of the original kept (a few flags per half, not a flag-fest).
- N7.2 When: pre-snap fouls (the play is not run), fouls during a run, a pass or a kick (the play is run and spoken as now, then the foul is announced at H2's hook). The announcer says what was flagged, on whom, with the player's surname from the roster ("a flag on the play, holding on the offense, <surname>"), then the choice.
- N7.3 The choice. When the player's team is the offended team: the prompt (in the style of 4.16 and 4.17) speaks the foul and the two outcomes in full ("if you accept: first and 10 on your 35; if you decline: second and 7 on your 42") and asks accept or decline, the answer starting at the better outcome by the computer's own rule, so a wrong key or Enter never hurts; Up, Down, a (accept), d (decline), Enter, F1 repeats the prompt with the situation. When the opponent is the offended team: the computer decides by rule (accept when the enforced outcome is better by its measure of down, distance, field position, clock and score; decline otherwise) and the announcer says what the opponent coach does. Both fouls by both teams on one play: offsetting, replay the down.
- N7.4 Enforcement is exact for the foul table: the half-distance rule, the spot, automatic first down, loss of down, replay, the touchdown and safety edge cases (a foul in the end zone), the interaction with a score, a turnover and a kick (a penalty on a kick-off or a punt, the two-point try, overtime).
