## 1. Overview
A turn-based, audio-only American football simulation for blind players. The player chooses five SAPI voices (announcer, own quarterback, opponent quarterback, own punter, opponent punter), types a coach name, picks a team for a 16-game season (NFL teams arranged in AFC and NFC divisions named in the strings: A. F. C. West, East, North and N. F. C. South among them; the exact count and list come from the strings in P2: 32 team nicknames, verified in P2: section 11), plays the game with the keyboard (running plays, line plunge, passes, screen pass, blitz, kick off, punt, field goal, extra point or two-point conversion, safety), then play-offs and a Super Bowl. Quarters have 25 to 35 plays; a two-minute warning comes before the end of the second and fourth quarter; about five plays follow it. Announcer commentary and jokes are spoken. [Source: football.txt and the exe's help strings; OBSERVED from text.] The code (S3.4, section 5.6) draws 30 to 37 plays per quarter and 5 to 7 plays after the two-minute warning, and plays the game as sections 4.14 to 5.12 describe; where the readme and the code differ, the code wins (D-027). [CODE (lifted)]
Special keys named in the program strings: F5 special key instructions, F6 game instructions, F7 football terminology and hints, F8 comments toggle, F11 adjust volume of voices and sounds, F12 change voice, Ctrl adjust rate of speech. ## 5. Mechanics (the schedule maker, standings, game clock, score speech, the play engine (5.5 to 5.13), the season engine and the play-offs (5.14 to 5.17))

### 5.1 Schedule maker (`makes`, p_42fa70) [CODE (lifted, S3.3; the constants and the picks checked in the assembly)]
Runs at a new season and when the player rejects the schedule in week 1. All draws are `Int(Rnd * N) + 1` (uniform, D-013).
1. Table of 16 weeks, each with opponent division, opponent team and home flag, all 0. `last` = Empty (counts as 0).
2. The player's division: for each of the three other teams of the division in team order (tn2 = 1 to 4 except the player's), twice (flag 1, then flag 2): draw a week 1 to 16 until it is free and not 1 or 2 weeks away from `last` (so the very first draw cannot give week 1 or 2, because `last` is 0; later draws can); `last` = that week; store (the player's division, the rival's team number, the flag). This puts the six division games in random weeks, never next to the previously placed one.
3. The 10 free weeks in week order: draw a division 1 to 8 until it is not the player's, draw a team 1 to 4, repeat both until that team is not yet on the schedule; draw a flag 1 or 2 until the count of that flag among these 10 games is below 5; store. Result: 8 games with flag 1 and 8 with flag 2, every opponent a different team, each division rival twice, 10 other teams once.
4. Write footbal2.dat: for week 1 to 16 the three values (opponent division, opponent team, flag) (section 12).
The numbers are Single Variants (Int of Rnd, vt 4, checked at 0x42fe0b) in the non-division games and Integer Variants in the division games (the For counters and tn1, vt 2, INFERRED); this only matters for the byte layout of the file (section 12).

### 5.2 Standings (`SayStand`) [CODE (lifted, S3.3; the sort checked in the assembly at 0x42da3b)]

### 5.3 Game clock (`sgc`, F4 and quarter starts) [CODE (lifted, S3.3; constants checked in the assembly)]
Arguments: played = plays played in the quarter, total = plays in this quarter (25 to 35), quarter = 1 to 4 (5 to 10 = over time). Time is made up from the play counter.
```
if played <> oldtime:                      oldtime starts Empty, which equals 0
    oldtime = played;  left = total - played
    pct = Int(left * 100 / total);  spread = 30; low = 15
    if quarter = 2 or quarter > 3:
        W = M2W (quarter 2) | M4W (quarter 4) | M5W (quarter > 4);  M2W, M4W, M5W = Int(Rnd * 3) + 5 = 5 to 7, set by pag
        if left < 5 + W:  CountDown = CountDown + W;  spread = 18; low = 9
        if left <= W:     CountDown = 125 if W = 5, 170 if W = 6, 215 if W = 7;  spread = 10; low = 5
    m = pct * 15 / CountDown;  mins = Int(m);  secs = Int((Int(m * 100) - mins * 100) * 60 / 100)
    draw j = Int(Rnd * spread) - low until 0 <= secs + j <= 59;  secs = secs + j
if quarter = 3 and played = 0:  mins = 15; secs = 0
text = " "; if mins > 0: text = Str$(mins) & " minute" & "s" if mins > 1
if secs > 0: text = text & " " & Str$(secs) & " second" & "s" if secs > 1
say5(text & " remaining in the " & (first, second, third, fourth, over time) & " quarter. ");  clk;  g = "112"
```
CountDown is 100 at the start of every quarter (pag, S3.4) and is saved in football.sav; the same play counter gives the same time again (oldtime), so F4 twice in one play repeats the minutes and seconds. Quirks in section 15. S7.3a (D-039, TESTED, `work/vb_variant_ops_s73a.py`): the numbers of this routine are Variants and the plays of a quarter (`total`, the argument 2) are a SINGLE Variant (`Int(Rnd * 8) + 30` is stored with FSTP float and the type word 4, asm 0x458328 to 0x458338), while the play counter is an Integer Variant; the automation library's VarSub, VarMul, VarDiv, VarAdd and VarInt, which the runtime's `__vbaVar*` helpers call, give a Single for a Single with an Integer or with another Single (only two Integers divided give a Double), so every operation of the chain (left, pct, m, Int(m * 100), the seconds) is a Single operation and its result is rounded to 32 bits before the next one. The clone rounds after every operation (`game/vbnum.single`). Over every input the game can give (total 30 to 37, played 0 to total, CountDown 100, 125, 170, 215 and 100 + n x W: 3,588 inputs) the minutes and seconds equal the library's; a Double chain differs in 57 (the first: total 30, played 10: Int(9.9 * 100) is 989 in a Single, so 53 seconds, and 990 in a Double, so 54).
