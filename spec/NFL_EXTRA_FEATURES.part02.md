## 8. NX3 QB keepers (according to NFL rules)

Reading (NQ1): designed quarterback runs and the NFL's treatment of a quarterback who runs.

Rule outline (checked by SX.0; corrected in 0.2):
- A quarterback who holds a live ball is a runner (3-27); his rushes and yards count as rushing, unless the play is a sack, which needs an apparent attempt to pass; a designed keeper stopped behind the line is a rush for a loss. Passer protections follow his "passing posture" and end when he becomes a runner; outside the pocket area he loses part of them. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 3-27, 12-2-9, 12-2-11; Guide for Statisticians pp. 11, 14 to 15]
- A runner who slides or dives feet or head first, or fakes it, is down the instant he touches the ground; defenders must pull up; unavoidable contact by a defender already committed is not a foul unless it is a forcible hit to the head or neck or other unnecessary roughness; late contact on a slid runner is unnecessary roughness, 15 yards, automatic first down. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 7-2-1(c) and (d), 12-2-8(d)]
- Intentional grounding: loss of down and 10 yards from the previous spot, or loss of down at the spot of the pass when that is worse, or a safety in the end zone; a passer outside the "pocket area" (the book's term, not "tackle box") who throws to or beyond the line of scrimmage is not grounding. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 8-2-1, 3-25]
- The quarterback sneak and the push (the "tush push") are legal in 2026: 12-1-4 bans pulling the runner, not pushing him; the 2025 ban vote failed 22 to 10 (24 needed); no ban was proposed in 2026. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 12-1-4; nfl.com]
- A fumble by a runner on fourth down, after the two-minute warning or on a try may be advanced only by the fumbler. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 8-7-5, 8-7-6; the game has no recovery by the offense, so it changes no result]
- Fumble and injury exposure of a running quarterback (the original has no injuries). Injuries are built in the first release by SX.8k but ONLY inside a Franchise (D-062, INJ-1): a quarterback's run in a quick game or a Classic season carries no injury risk; in a franchise game it is one of the plays that can hurt its runner (INJ-3).

Requirements:
- N3.1 New offense plays, each a named play with an outcome row against the six defense calls (data, H8): QB Sneak (short yardage, high success, tiny fumble chance, the right play on third and fourth and short), QB Keeper (a bootleg or naked keeper to the outside, medium yards, a chance of a loss that counts as a rush for a loss, not a sack), QB Draw (a pass look that becomes a run, strong against a blitz, weak against a short run defense), and the Scramble (a called pass play that, instead of the incompletion or the sack, ends with the quarterback running; it extends the original's drop-back sentences "scrambles left looking for a receiver" and "is forced out of the pocket", which today lead to ordinary pass results). The tables are SX.1's design (NFL_EXTRAS_DESIGN 7.1, start values), calibrated in SX.5 from NFL quarterback rushing statistics (NFL_RULES_RESEARCH Part 1.6 and Part 9 section 2: designed runs 3.66 yards, scrambles 7.67, third or fourth and 1 converted 83.4 percent).
- N3.2 The slide: when a keeper gains a first down or is near the sideline or the goal line, a slide may be chosen by the quarterback (an automatic rule for the computer; for the player a quarterback who runs the play slides when the situation says so, announced, never a prompt in the middle of a play).
- N3.3 Statistics: the keeper counts as a running play and its yards as running yards, plus its own line in the added statistics (C7); a fumble counts as a fumble lost when lost.
- N3.4 Sentences in the manner of 5.9 ("<quarterback> keeps the ball and runs around the right end", "the quarterback sneak is good for a first down"), a new phrase family with the original's "never repeat one of the last N" rule, customizable (C5).
- N3.5 The computer chooses keepers (short-yardage sneaks, late-game scrambles when trailing, rarely otherwise) by rules in its design file; the defense calls against a keeper follow 5.7's matching logic extended to the new plays.
- Done when: the table values and the rule tests pass, every slide case is tested, a seeded long run's statistics are inside the NFL ranges the design file names, and the Classic golden test passes.

## 9. NX4 Onside kicks (according to NFL rules)

Rule outline (checked by SX.0; corrected in 0.2):
- 2024: the dynamic kickoff; an onside kick had to be declared and only a team trailing in the fourth quarter could attempt it. 2025: any quarter, still trailing. [SRC-SECONDARY; the 2025 book was not found. The "34-yard line" of 2025 press reports does not appear in the 2026 book; UNVERIFIED which 2025 line it meant.]
- 2026: "at any time during the game" the kicking team may declare an onside kick; there is no trailing, score or period condition anywhere in the book. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 6-1-6]
- Declaration: to the Referee before the 25-second play clock starts; the receivers are told, so there is no surprise onside kick. A declared kick that goes untouched beyond the receivers' setup zone is unsportsmanlike conduct, 15 yards, the receivers' ball. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 6-1-6]
- Kick spot: the kicking team's 35 for a kick-off, its 20 for a safety kick; alignment: every kicking player but the kicker and holder with a front foot on the line, at most five on each side of the ball; receivers: restraining line 10 yards ahead, eight or nine in the 15-yard onside setup zone. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 6-1-6(b) to (h), 6-1-2, 11-5-2]
- Recovery: the kickers may touch or recover only after the ball touches a receiver or reaches the receivers' restraining line (10 yards); early touching is 5 yards or the receivers' ball at the spot; a legal recovery by the kickers is their ball; an untouched ball at rest is the receivers' ball. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 6-1-6(e) and (f), 6-2-5]
- After a safety the team scored upon kicks from its 20, and an onside kick is allowed under the same declaration rule. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 11-5-2, 6-1-1(b), 6-1-6(b)]
- Other 2026 kickoff rules (the setup zone, a touch back after a kick from the 50 comes out to the 20) are not part of this feature; the original's kick-off stays in both modes (design section 4, D-042). [SRC-OFFICIAL, 6-1-3, 6-1-5]
- Recovery rate of declared onside kicks: 2024 3 of 50 (6.0 percent), 2025 5 of 52 (9.6 percent), 2026 to week 5 none of 11; every attempt came in the fourth quarter from the trailing team. [SRC-SECONDARY, computed from public play-by-play data, NFL_RULES_RESEARCH Part 1.6]

Requirements:
- N4.1 At a kick-off or safety kick of the player's team in N. F. L. rules mode, a yes-or-no question in the style of 4.16: "<coach> do you wish to try an onside kick" with the answer starting at no. The 2026 rule allows it at every free kick, so (OWNER DECISION 2026-10-14, D-041) it is asked only when it makes sense: the player's team trails in the third or fourth quarter, or leads by 1 to 8 points with at most 5:00 left in the fourth quarter; a setting "Onside question: always" (off by default) asks at every kick. Classic: never asked. AMENDED 2026-10-20 (D-085, the owner: "I did not see onside kicks in there. My version of the game must follow current 2026 nfl rules."): the question is asked at EVERY kick-off and safety kick of the player's team in N. F. L. rules mode (the setting's default is "always"; a stored "sensible" the player never chose becomes "always" once; "sensible" stays as the player's own choice); built in SX.2b; SX.10d turns it into the 2026 kick-off menu (deep kick first, short kick into the landing zone, squib, onside).
- N4.2 The kick: a short kick sentence sequence in the manner of 5.10 (the setup, the kick, the scramble for the ball), with the original's kick sounds, and one outcome draw with the recovery chance from the rules table (kept as data with its source and year, N4.4): recovered by the kicking team at a spot, or the receiving team gets it at a spot drawn as a short distance from the kick spot (and a rare long return for the receiving team).
- N4.3 Penalties on the kick (offside, illegal touching, illegal alignment) use NX7's machinery if NX7 is on; if not, they are not simulated.
- N4.4 The computer: declares onside kicks by a rule table (trailing in the fourth quarter by the time left and the deficit, design 8.4). The 2026 rule allows an onside kick when leading or tied, declared like any other (no surprise kick exists), but no NFL team tried one in 2024 to 2026, so the computer never does; the player may. The recovery probability and the decision thresholds are data with their reason.
- N4.5 Statistics: onside kicks tried and recovered (C7).
- Done when: the rule tests match the 2026 wording SX.0 records, a scripted game shows the player declining, the player kicking and recovering, the player kicking and losing, and the computer kicking, and the Classic golden test passes.

## 12. NX7 Penalties (accept or decline, according to NFL rules)

Rule outline (checked by SX.0, NFL_RULES_RESEARCH Parts 6 and 7; corrected in 0.2; the list is data and can grow):
- The offended team may decline any penalty unless a rule forbids it, and play goes on as if there were no foul; it may also accept the penalty and decline only its yardage. One captain indicates the choice after the Referee explains it (the rulebook names no coach; the game lets the player, as head coach, decide). The choice is final once the ball is ready, except that a head coach may change it after a replay reversal. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 14-1-1, 18-1-3, 15-7-3]
- Automatic first down is the default for every foul by the defense on a scrimmage down, except ten listed fouls (offside, encroachment, neutral zone infraction, delay of game, illegal substitution, excess timeout, running into the kicker, more than 11 in the formation or at the snap, defensive illegal formation on a scrimmage kick). Offense fouls keep the down unless they carry a loss of down (the combination penalties: illegal forward pass, intentional grounding and a few others); an enforcement that reaches the line to gain is first and 10. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 14-1-2 Items 2, 4, 5]
- Spots: previous, succeeding, spot of the foul, dead ball, basic; runs by "three-and-one"; passes from the previous spot except pass interference and personal fouls; the half-distance rule with its exceptions (spot fouls, intentional grounding at the spot of the pass, palpably unfair acts, safety by enforcement). One penalty per team per down, chosen by the offended team; double fouls offset and the down is replayed. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 3-13-3, 14-2-1, 14-3, 14-4, 14-5-1]
- Dead and live: false start, encroachment and neutral zone infraction are whistled dead before the snap; offside, illegal formation, motion and shift are called at the snap with the play run (the 0.1 word "pre-snap" covered both). [SRC-OFFICIAL, 7-4, 14-4-1, 14-4-9]
- Clock: after a penalty the clock starts as if there had been no foul, but on the snap after the first-half warning, in the last five minutes of the SECOND HALF (not "the fourth quarter and overtime" as 0.1 said), for an offense foul in the fourth period or regular-season overtime after the ball was ready, and after a second delay; the 10-second runoff exists only after the two-minute warning of either half; there is no "inside one minute" rule. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 4-3-2(e), 4-7]
- Scores: a defense personal or unsportsmanlike foul on a touchdown play is enforced on the try or the kick-off; an accepted offense foul erases a score; an accepted defensive foul on the last play of a half gives one untimed down. [SRC-OFFICIAL, 14-2-3, 4-8-2(a)]
- The list to start from (design 9.2, 27 rows with the 2025 frequency mix): false start, delay of game, offside, neutral zone infraction, encroachment, illegal formation, illegal shift, offensive and defensive holding, illegal use of hands, illegal contact, pass interference (both), roughing the passer, intentional grounding, ineligible receiver downfield, face mask, unnecessary roughness, illegal block in the back, horse collar, unsportsmanlike conduct, running into and roughing the kicker, kick-off offside, holding on a return, too many men on the field, illegal touching of an onside kick. [SRC-OFFICIAL for the yards and spots; the frequencies SRC-SECONDARY, Part 8]

Requirements:
- N7.1 The foul table is data, with a permanent ID per foul: name, spoken text, who commits it, the phases it can happen in (before the snap, run, pass, kick), yards, automatic first down, loss of down, enforcement spot, whether the down is replayed, and its base chance per play type. Frequencies are calibrated to NFL accepted-penalty averages per game and per team (SX.0 records the sources and the average per play), with the quarter's feel of the original kept (a few flags per half, not a flag-fest).
- N7.2 When: pre-snap fouls (the play is not run), fouls during a run, a pass or a kick (the play is run and spoken as now, then the foul is announced at H2's hook). The announcer says what was flagged, on whom, with the player's surname from the roster ("a flag on the play, holding on the offense, <surname>"), then the choice.
- N7.3 The choice. When the player's team is the offended team: the prompt (in the style of 4.16 and 4.17) speaks the foul and the two outcomes in full ("if you accept: first and 10 on your 35; if you decline: second and 7 on your 42") and asks accept or decline, the answer starting at the better outcome by the computer's own rule, so a wrong key or Enter never hurts; Up, Down, a (accept), d (decline), Enter, F1 repeats the prompt with the situation. When the opponent is the offended team: the computer decides by rule (accept when the enforced outcome is better by its measure of down, distance, field position, clock and score; decline otherwise) and the announcer says what the opponent coach does. Both fouls by both teams on one play: offsetting, replay the down.
- N7.4 Enforcement is exact for the foul table: the half-distance rule, the spot, automatic first down, loss of down, replay, the touchdown and safety edge cases (a foul in the end zone), the interaction with a score, a turnover and a kick (a penalty on a kick-off or a punt, the two-point try, overtime).
- N7.5 Customization: the foul table's spoken texts and the on/off and weight of each foul are in `penalties.txt` in the `customize` folder (C5, 7.9).
- N7.6 Interactions are listed and tested: timeouts (NX1: the game refuses a timeout it cannot grant, so no timeout foul arises), challenges (NX2: judgment fouls are never reviewable, but penalty administration is, and a reversal can change the enforcement and lets the coach choose again, 15-7), keepers (NX3: the slide and the late hit), onside kicks (NX4: early touching), the clock (C2: runoff and clock restarts).
- N7.7 Calibration target (D-042): 12.8 accepted fouls per game, band 12.0 to 13.6 (the 2024 and 2025 seasons; the early-2026 value of 15.1 is a 79-game sample) [SRC-SECONDARY, NFL_RULES_RESEARCH Part 1.6].
- Done when: every foul in the table has an enforcement test against the rule table, the offsetting and half-distance cases are tested, a scripted game shows the player accepting, declining, and being the fouling team, the frequency check over 10,000 simulated games falls inside the NFL averages the design file names, and the Classic golden test passes.
